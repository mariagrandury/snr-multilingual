# `snr_definition/` — SNR variants × decision accuracy on Apertus

> Step-by-step plan for refreshing / extending this analysis:
> [INSTRUCTIONS.md](INSTRUCTIONS.md). Use one Claude session per
> research question — see [../PARALLEL_SESSIONS.md](../PARALLEL_SESSIONS.md).

This directory holds per-task SNR/DA tables and the SNR-vs-DA correlation
plots, generated by:

- [`multilingual/run_apertus_snr_variants.py`](../../multilingual/run_apertus_snr_variants.py) — writes `snr_variants_per_task.csv`
- [`multilingual/analyze_snr_variants.py`](../../multilingual/analyze_snr_variants.py) — reads the CSV, writes everything else

The 12 Apertus runs are `apertus-{175M,350M,600M,1B}-fwEdu{30,60,90}-fw…-seed1904`.
SNR is computed from per-mix last-5 ckpts; DA is decision accuracy (rank
agreement) over 3 mixes per (size or ckpt) pair.

## Headline answers

> The HF parquet ships both per-language aggregates (e.g.
> `global_mmlu_full_ar`) and per-(lang, subject) facets
> (`global_mmlu_full_ar_anatomy`, …). The cluster pipeline used to fold
> the facets into the parent via `aggregate_parents`. To match that
> semantics, `run_apertus_snr_variants.py` now applies a **parent-only
> task filter** (`_is_parent_task`) before computing variants — so the
> CSV has 104 rows, identical to the historical scope. Without the
> filter, per-language Pearson r averaged over ~57 subject-facet
> replicates of `global_mmlu_full_<lang>` per language and was
> dominated by whatever variant tracked the subject decomposition
> (gave `mpd` @ r ≈ +0.25 mean as a spurious headline). The numbers
> below come from the parent-only rerun.

- **Global best variant** (mean Pearson r across 12 languages,
  DA-size): **`rms_deviation`** at r ≈ **+0.273**, in a six-way tie
  with the dispersion cluster (`mpd`, `dispersion`, `range`,
  `quartile_deviation`, `aad`) all at +0.270 ± 0.003. These six are
  algebraically near-equivalent at n_mixes = 3 (see Key insights).
- **Worst variants**: `tukey` (+0.004) and `projection` (+0.028) —
  depth-based aggregators don't track DA on Apertus. `mad` (+0.150)
  also underperforms — outlier-robust spread isn't useful with only 3
  mixes.
- **Best variant per language** (DA-size Pearson r, pooled across the 3
  small sizes) — full table:
  [`best_variant_per_language.csv`](best_variant_per_language.csv):

  | lang | best variant | r | runner-up | r |
  |---|---|---:|---|---:|
  | es | `star_discrepancy` | +0.666 | `discrepancy` | +0.646 |
  | ru | `rel_mpd` | +0.663 | `rel_dispersion` | +0.663 |
  | hi | `rel_std` | +0.599 | `rel_mpd` | +0.581 |
  | sw | `dispersion_shifted` | +0.522 | `star_discrepancy` | +0.515 |
  | tr | `mad` | +0.507 | `mpsd` | +0.476 |
  | eu | `dist_std` | +0.427 | `aad` | +0.392 |
  | vi | `discrepancy` | +0.423 | `dispersion_shifted` | +0.418 |
  | th | `iqr` | +0.377 | `rel_dispersion` | +0.377 |
  | ar | `dist_std` | +0.286 | `aad` | +0.272 |
  | ja | `projection` | +0.249 | `mad` | +0.247 |
  | en | `discrepancy` | +0.146 | `tukey` | +0.114 |
  | zh | `star_discrepancy_shifted` | +0.053 | `dispersion_shifted` | −0.011 |

  Per-language r values are 2–4× stronger than under the contaminated
  scope, and the winners cluster meaningfully:
  - **Spread family** (`dist_std`, `aad`, `dispersion_shifted`,
    `mpsd`): wins for ar/eu/sw/tr — these are languages where mix
    spread is the cleanest signal.
  - **Discrepancy family** (`discrepancy`, `star_discrepancy`,
    `rel_star_discrepancy`): wins for es/vi/en — empirical-CDF
    distance is best here.
  - **Relative spread** (`rel_std`, `rel_mpd`, `rel_dispersion`): wins
    for hi/ru — the small absolute-spread tasks need normalisation.
  - **Robust** (`mad`, `iqr`): wins for tr/th — these languages have a
    couple of outlier benchmarks per mix and the median-based variants
    handle them.
  - `zh` and `en` remain the weakest correlations (r < 0.15); SNR
    is a poor DA proxy on those subsets and we should not use it as a
    reliability filter for English-only benchmarks.

- **Top benchmarks per language**, scored by `snr_rms_deviation_1B`
  ([`top_benchmarks_per_language.csv`](top_benchmarks_per_language.csv)):

  ![Top-5 benchmarks per language by SNR (rms_deviation @ 1B)](top_benchmarks_per_language.png)

  `multiblimp_<lang>` remains the SNR leader for every language where
  it exists (ar, en, es, eu, hi, ru, tr). The dominance is now
  smaller in magnitude (e.g. `multiblimp_hin` ≈ 11.4 vs the next
  Hindi benchmark at ≈ 1.9 — vs ~17.0 under the contaminated scope)
  because the headline variant changed from the absolute-spread `mpd`
  to `rms_deviation`, which compresses the multiblimp outlier. When
  multiblimp is unavailable, `xstorycloze_<lang>` typically tops the
  list with `hellaswag_<lang>` second.

Family rollup of the per-language winners (n = 10 covered languages):

| family | DA-size langs | DA-ckpt langs |
|---|---|---|
| dispersion   | ar, eu | ar, hi, tr |
| discrepancy  | en, es, sw, vi, zh | es, ja, zh |
| rel_spread   | hi, ru, th | eu, ru, vi |
| robust (mad) | tr | en |
| depth        | ja | sw, th |

**Stable under both DA flavors** (same family wins for size and ckpt):
es and zh stay in `discrepancy`; ru stays in `rel_spread`; ar stays in
`dispersion`. Those four languages are the easiest to "trust SNR for"
— they pick the same kind of aggregator regardless of which DA you
use.

**No clear typological clustering.** The 4 stable languages span
Semitic (ar), Romance (es), Slavic-Cyrillic (ru), and Sinitic-Han
(zh). The `dispersion` group under DA-size is {ar, eu} — Arabic and
Basque, no obvious shared trait beyond both being non-Indo-European.
The `discrepancy` group is the largest under DA-size (en, es, sw, vi,
zh) and contains four of the five Latin-script languages with
non-multiblimp coverage; that's a tighter clue but the n is too small
to claim "Latin-script ⇒ discrepancy variants." The most defensible
generalisation is just: **most variant families work somewhere, no
single family dominates more than 5/12 languages**.

Using the global-best DA-size variant **`rms_deviation`** at 1B:

Numbers in [`top_benchmarks_per_language.csv`](top_benchmarks_per_language.csv).
Each bar is annotated with `DA-s` (DA-size at 600M → 1B) and `DA-c`
(mean DA-ckpt across early-step pairs at 1B).

Patterns:
- **`multiblimp_<lang>` is rank-1 in every language where it exists**
  (ar, en, es, eu, hi, ru, tr) — by 2-5× the runner-up. Both DA-size
  and DA-ckpt are typically 1.0 (perfect rank agreement) on these
  rows.
- **Without multiblimp**, the top slot is consistently
  `xstorycloze_<lang>` (sw, th, vi, zh) or `hellaswag_<lang>` (ja).
- **DA-size = 0** (zero rank agreement) appears for `xnli_<lang>` rows
  in ar/en/hi at small sizes — high SNR there is misleading because
  XNLI's mix ranking diverges from the 1B target. These are
  benchmarks the SNR-for-reliability framing should de-prioritise.
- **DA-ckpt is typically ≥ DA-size**: the per-language top tasks
  agree with the within-size early-vs-late ckpt rank more often than
  with the cross-size 600M-vs-1B rank.

The takeaway for benchmark selection: **use `rms_deviation` SNR at 1B
as the screen, then drop benchmarks where DA-size = 0** to filter out
high-SNR-but-misranked cases (mostly `xnli_<lang>` here).

## Key insights

- **Several variants are algebraically equivalent at n_mixes = 3.** With only 3 mixes the dispersion-family signals (`dispersion`, `range`, `mpd`, `quartile_deviation`, `rms_deviation`, `aad`) are linearly proportional, so their `log10(SNR)` correlate at r ≥ 0.999 and produce identical Pearson r against DA. Treat them as a single variant. Two more redundancy clusters in `variant_correlation_matrix.png`: {`iqr`, `rel_dispersion`, `rel_mpd`, `rel_std`} (r ≥ 0.998) and {`discrepancy`, `star_discrepancy`} (r = 0.959).
- **DA is 4-level discrete in this setup** (3 mixes ⇒ DA ∈ {0, ⅓, ⅔, 1}). All Pearson r values are computed over a quantized y-axis, so absolute r magnitudes are capped well below what 25-mix DataDecide reports. Treat the values as ordinal evidence, not ground-truth correlations.
- **Best global variants are in the dispersion cluster.** With the parent-only filter, `rms_deviation` / `mpd` / `dispersion` / `range` / `quartile_deviation` / `aad` all tie at mean Pearson r ≈ +0.270 across languages. The dispersion family is a sound default; if you want a single canonical name, prefer `rms_deviation` (it's identical here and is the more recognisable statistic outside this codebase).
- **Best variant per language differs by up to 5×.** With the parent-only scope:
  - `es`, `ru`, `hi`: r ≥ +0.60 (the strongest correlations) — the discrepancy and relative-spread families.
  - `sw`, `tr`, `eu`, `vi`, `th`: r in [+0.38, +0.52] — spread, robust, or relative-spread families.
  - `ar`, `ja`: r in [+0.25, +0.29].
  - `en`, `zh`: r < +0.15 — SNR is a poor DA proxy here, regardless of variant.
- **DA-size and DA-ckpt disagree on a few variants.** `star_discrepancy_shifted`, `mad`, `gini`, `tukey`, `dispersion_shifted`, `star_discrepancy` all have r ~0.08–0.12 higher under DA-ckpt than DA-size. Pick DA-ckpt when the goal is "stop training early without losing the right mix"; DA-size when extrapolating to a larger model.
- **`rel_std_snr` is no longer degenerate.** After the fix that broadcasts cross-mix std as `data_noise`, `snr_rel_std_<size>` spans roughly [0.06, 9] (was [0.97, 1.05]). It's now a reasonable variant; in this dataset it sits in the redundancy cluster with `iqr`/`rel_mpd`/`rel_dispersion` and wins for `hi`.
- **`projection_snr` and `tukey_snr` are mostly uncorrelated with DA** under DA-size (mean r ≤ +0.03) — the depth-based formulations don't track decision accuracy here. Avoid for Apertus, except `projection` happens to win for `ja` (r = +0.25).
- **Per-language coverage is uneven.** de, fr, th have ≤4 valid (task, size) cells and are skipped from the per-language plots (ja additionally for DA-ckpt). Don't draw conclusions from those languages without evaluating more benchmarks first.
