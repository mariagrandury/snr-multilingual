\section{Surrogates of Decision Accuracy: Definitions}
\label{app:surrogates}

A \emph{surrogate} is a statistic of a benchmark that is computed on the proxy size alone. It is meant to tell, without training the reference, whether the ranking of the proxy can be trusted.
This appendix defines every surrogate we compute. These are the signal-to-noise ratio of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} with all its signal and noise variants (\S\ref{app:sur-snr}--\ref{app:sur-noise}), the task-selection criteria of FineTasks (\S\ref{app:sur-finetasks}), and a catalogue of statistics from the literature on evaluation reliability (\S\ref{app:sur-catalogue}).
For each we give the formula as implemented, the paper it comes from, and, where the two differ, the difference.
\S\ref{app:sur-scoring} describes how surrogates are scored and \S\ref{app:sur-results} gives the summary table of correlations.

\subsection{Notation and the truths}
\label{app:sur-notation}

A task is scored on the $V$ design variants of one proxy size.
Variant $a$ has a training curve $x_a(t)$ at the ten evaluated tenths $t \in \{0.1,\dots,1\}$ of its run, a final score $x_a = x_a(1)$, and a \emph{noise window} $W_a = \{x_a(t) : t \in \{0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 1\}\}$ with mean $\bar{w}_a$ and standard deviation $s_a$.
$\mathcal{P}$ is the set of variant pairs (every pair, or the pairs that differ in one design axis), $n$ the number of test items, $c$ the chance level, $\bar{x}$ the mean final score over variants, and $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function.
Scores are oriented so that higher is better (bits per byte enter with a flipped sign).

\paragraph{Decision accuracy.} For two score vectors $u$ and $y$ over the same variants,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DA}(u, y) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{(a,b) \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbf{1}\big[\mathrm{sign}(u_a - u_b) = \mathrm{sign}(y_a - y_b)\big],
\label{eq:sur-da}
\end{equation}
the definition of \citet{magnusson_datadecide_2025} and \citet{heineman_signal_2025}.
A surrogate is scored against three truths. DA-size is $\mathrm{DA}(x^{\text{proxy}}, x^{\text{ref}})$ between the final rankings of the proxy and of the reference. DA-goal is $\mathrm{DA}(x^{\text{proxy}}(t), x^{\text{ref}})$ for $t < 1$. DA-ckpt is $\mathrm{DA}(x^{\text{proxy}}(t), x^{\text{proxy}})$, the proxy against its own final checkpoint.
Each is also measured as Kendall's $\tau_b$ and Spearman's $\rho$ on the same two vectors.

\emph{Differences from the source.}
\citet{magnusson_datadecide_2025} take the mean of three seeds as the target and average the decision accuracy of three single-seed proxies. Our grid has one seed per variant on both sides.
Neither paper treats ties, and \citet{heineman_signal_2025} derive $\tau = 2\,\mathrm{DA} - 1$ under that assumption.
Under Eq.~\ref{eq:sur-da}, a pair tied on both sides agrees and a pair tied on one side is a miss. So with $C$ concordant, $D$ discordant, $T_2$ doubly tied and $T_1$ singly tied pairs, $2\,\mathrm{DA} - 1 = \tau_a + (T_2 - T_1)/|\mathcal{P}|$.

\subsection{The signal-to-noise ratio}
\label{app:sur-snr}

\citet{heineman_signal_2025} define, for the final scores $m_j$ of the small models and the last $n_c$ checkpoints $m_{j,1},\dots,m_{j,n_c}$ of each,
\begin{equation}
\text{Signal} = \frac{\max_{j,k} |m_j - m_k|}{\bar{m}}, \qquad
\text{Noise}(m_j) = \frac{1}{\bar{m}_j}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_c - 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_c}(m_{j,i} - \bar{m}_j)^2}, \qquad
\mathrm{SNR} = \frac{\text{Signal}}{\text{Noise}},
\label{eq:sur-snr-paper}
\end{equation}
with the noise averaged over the small models and $n_c = 5$.
They report $R = 0.791$ ($R^2 = 0.626$) between this SNR and decision accuracy over their benchmarks.

We compute the SNR with the code released with that paper, which differs from Eq.~\ref{eq:sur-snr-paper} in three ways.
With $s^{0}_a$ the population (divide by $n_c$) standard deviation of $W_a$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Signal}_{\text{code}} = \frac{\max_{a,b} |x_a - x_b|}{\frac{1}{V}\sum_a \bar{w}_a}, \qquad
\text{Noise}_{\text{code}} = \frac{\frac{1}{V}\sum_a s^{0}_a}{\frac{1}{V}\sum_a \bar{w}_a}.
\label{eq:sur-snr-code}
\end{equation}
(i) The signal is divided by the mean of the window means, not by the mean of the final scores.
(ii) The noise is a ratio of means over models, not a mean of per-model ratios.
(iii) The standard deviation divides by $n_c$, not $n_c - 1$, which makes the noise smaller by $\sqrt{4/5}$ at $n_c = 5$ and the SNR larger by 1.118.
Our window is five checkpoints spread over the last 20\,\% of the run, where the original uses the last five saved checkpoints.

\label{app:sur-signals}

Each is divided by a noise of \S\ref{app:sur-noise} to give an SNR.
Table~3 of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} prints a formula for each. The last column records where the code computes something else.

Appendix A.4 of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} also names five measures in prose (variance, mean distance, relative standard deviation, star discrepancy, dispersion). They correspond to \texttt{rms\_deviation} squared, \texttt{mpd}, \texttt{rel\_std}, \texttt{star\_discrepancy} and \texttt{dispersion}.
In their Table~3 the $R^2$ with decision accuracy is 0.57 for relative dispersion and relative standard deviation, 0.55 for relative mean pairwise distance, 0.48 for the interquartile range, and below 0.10 for the Gini, discrepancy and depth signals.

\subsection{Noise variants}
\label{app:sur-noise}

Six noises enter the signal $\times$ noise grid ($22 \times 6 = 132$ ratios, each signal and each noise also scored alone).

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Checkpoint noise, relative} (\texttt{ckpt\_rel}): $\text{Noise}_{\text{code}}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:sur-snr-code}, the checkpoint-to-checkpoint noise of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} as released.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint noise, absolute} (\texttt{ckpt\_abs}): $\frac{1}{V}\sum_a s^0_a$, their Eq.~7, $\sigma(\{U(t_j)\}_{j=T-n_c+1}^{T})$, averaged over variants.
\item \textbf{Depth noises} (\texttt{tukey\_depth}, \texttt{projection\_depth}): $\frac{1}{V}\sum_a s^0_a$ divided by the mean depth of the window means $\bar{w}_a$ among the final scores. These are the noises the released code pairs with the two depth signals. The paper does not print them.
\item \textbf{Benchmark noise, relative} (\texttt{kfold\_rel}): the mean over models of the relative standard deviation of a model's score across $k = 5$ disjoint folds of the test items,
\begin{equation}
\text{Noise}_{k\text{-fold}} = \frac{1}{V}\sum_a \frac{1}{x_a}\sqrt{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m_{a,i} - x_a)^2},
\label{eq:sur-kfold}
\end{equation}
with $m_{a,i}$ the fold scores. This is our own definition. It needs one evaluation run and no intermediate checkpoint.
The ladder report carries no per-item outputs, so we use its expectation over random partitions. For $n$ binary items at accuracy $p$, a fold is a sample of $n/k$ items without replacement, hence
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m_i - p)^2\Big] = \frac{p(1-p)}{n/k}\cdot\frac{n - n/k}{n - 1} = \frac{p(1-p)(k-1)}{n-1},
\label{eq:sur-kfold-closed}
\end{equation}
and the noise of a model is the root of this divided by $p$. The value of $k$ rescales every task alike and moves no ranking.
\item \textbf{Benchmark noise, absolute} (\texttt{kfold\_abs}): Eq.~\ref{eq:sur-kfold} without the division by $x_a$.
\end{itemize}

\citet{heineman_signal_2025} define three further noises that validate the checkpoint noise and do not enter the grid.
\emph{Seed noise} is $\sigma(M)$ over runs that differ in the seed, with $M_i = \frac{1}{n_c}\sum_{j=T-n_c+1}^{T} U(t_j)$. \emph{Data-order noise} is the same over runs that differ in the order of documents.
\emph{Total variation} is $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}|U(t) - U(t-1)| - \frac{1}{T}(U(T) - U(0))$.
They report $R^2$ of 0.82, 0.86 and 0.95 between these and the checkpoint noise.
On our ladder the seed noise is the sample standard deviation of the final score over the three seeds of the replicated cells. We use it to size effects, not as a surrogate.
In the terms of \citet{wang_measuring_2025}, checkpoint and seed noise are prediction noise, the $k$-fold noise is data noise, and a training curve never shows the latter because it re-evaluates the same items.

\subsection{The FineTasks criteria}
\label{app:sur-finetasks}

FineTasks \citep{kydlicek_finetasks_nodate} selects tasks with four criteria computed along training, defined with formulas in the FineWeb2 paper \citep{penedo_fineweb2_2025}.
With $M$ the runs, $m(s)$ the score of run $m$ at step $s$, $\sigma_s$ the standard deviation over runs at step $s$ and $b$ the random baseline:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.14\linewidth}p{0.33\linewidth}p{0.47\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{As implemented} & \textbf{Against the source} \\
\midrule
Monotonicity & $\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{m}\rho_{\text{Spearman}}(s, m(s)) \geq 0.5$ & matches \\
Low noise (``SNR'') & $\dfrac{\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_m m(1)}{\frac{1}{S}\sum_s \sigma_s} > 20$ & the blog's definition. The FineWeb2 paper uses $\frac{1}{S}\sum_s \mu_s/\sigma_s$, with $\sigma_s$ the population deviation over four seed runs. Ours is the sample deviation over the design variants \\
Non-randomness & $\dfrac{\max_m m(1) - b}{\sigma_{1}} > 3$ & the source averages $\sigma_s$ over the last five steps and takes it from the seed runs \\
Ordering consistency & mean $\tau_a$ of the run rankings at consecutive steps in the second half of training & matches. The source sets no threshold \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The FineTasks criteria (\texttt{finetasks\_criteria.py}). Their ``noise'' is the spread across runs at one step, which the SNR framework calls signal: their SNR is the inverse of our relative signal, averaged over the run.}
\label{tab:sur-finetasks}
\end{table}

The first analysis of statistics beyond SNR (\texttt{analyze.py}, \texttt{finetasks\_criteria.py}) also scored the following proxy-only statistics, which have no external source. They are the $R^2$ of the linear fit of the score on $\log N$ over the rungs up to the proxy, the margin $\bar{x} - c$ above chance, and the proxy's own DA-ckpt at 10\,\%, 50\,\% and 90\,\%. They are also the gain of the mean score between 50\,\% (or 90\,\%) and the final checkpoint, the slope of the mean score over the last three tenths, and the earliest tenth from which $\tau_a$ with the final ranking stays at or above 0.8. Finally, they are the mean $\tau_a$ between the rankings inside the noise window, the variance of the rank of a variant over the second half of training, the standard deviation of the final scores across variants, and the item count.

\subsection{The catalogue of statistics from the literature}
\label{app:sur-catalogue}

Tables~\ref{tab:sur-separation}--\ref{tab:sur-agreement} define the catalogue (\texttt{catalogue.py}).
Most of these statistics were proposed for a different unit of replication, such as test items, topics, seeds or public models.
We port each one to what one proxy size offers, that is, the checkpoints of its noise window and the rungs below it. The last column states what we changed.
``Own'' marks a statistic that the cited paper motivates but does not define.
Throughout, $s_a$ is the sample standard deviation of $W_a$, $s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{V}\sum_a s_a^2}$ the pooled one, $d_{ab} = |x_a - x_b|$, $z_{ab} = d_{ab}/\sqrt{s_a^2 + s_b^2}$ (capped at 50), and $\mathrm{se}_p = \sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$ the binomial standard error \citep{miller_adding_2024}.
$\mathrm{MS_B}$ and $\mathrm{MS_W}$ are the between-variant and within-variant mean squares of the $V \times 5$ matrix of window scores.

\paragraph{Statistics we cannot compute.}
The ladder report holds one aggregate score per checkpoint.
Statistics of per-item outputs or log-probabilities are therefore out of reach. These include the proxy metrics of \citet{magnusson_datadecide_2025} (correct probability, normalised correct probability, margin) and the transformation chain of \citet{schaeffer_why_2024}. They also include item response models \citep{polo_tinybenchmarks_2024, kipnis_metabench_2025}, paired significance tests, the almost stochastic order of \citet{dror_deep_2019} and prompt-format sensitivity \citep{alzahrani_when_2024}. Finally, they include the minimum detectable ability difference of \citet{yauney_reliable_2026}, which also needs the reference, and the token-level proxies of \citet{patel_forecasting_2026} and \citet{koh_predicting_2026}.

\paragraph{Correlations reported by the sources.}
These set the scale for our own numbers, but each is measured on a different population.
\citet{heineman_signal_2025}: SNR against decision accuracy, $R = 0.791$.
\citet{bhagia_establishing_2025}: the standard deviation of the task loss over the last ten checkpoints against the error of the accuracy prediction, $r = 0.821$.
\citet{liu_regmix_2025}: a regression fitted on 1M-parameter models predicts the ranking of data mixtures at 1B parameters with Spearman 0.97, on validation loss.
\citet{madaan_quantifying_2024}: monotonicity is 0.09 for MMLU and 0.95 for its cloze formulation.
\citet{lourie_scaling_2025} find that downstream scaling laws extrapolate in 39\,\% of cases, and \citet{wang_can_2026} that proxy rankings reverse under small changes of hyperparameters.

\subsection{How a surrogate is scored}
\label{app:sur-scoring}

Surrogates are read on the proxy only: its final checkpoint, its ten tenths, its noise window and the rungs below it.
The single exception is \texttt{pseudo\_ref\_da}.
A cell is a (task, proxy size, fraction of the run). The truth is taken over cells above chance and with at least three pairs.
For each truth and each surrogate, we report Spearman's $\rho$ over one point per (benchmark, language) cluster. Each point is the mean surrogate of the cluster against its mean truth, pooled over proxy sizes and checkpoints.
A task and its reformulated twins form one cluster, which removes the repeated measures of a task. Clusters of one benchmark still share its design (item count, answer format), so the $p$-value is a screen and not an exact test. Below 30 clusters, the $p$-value comes from a permutation test.
The Benjamini-Hochberg correction runs over every configuration of the search (truth $\times$ subset $\times$ surrogate). The only exception is the two-statistic threshold filters, which are built from the surrogates that rank highest on the same truth.
The \emph{ceiling} of a truth is its $\rho$ with itself when the reference is read at 90\,\% of its run. No surrogate can be expected to track a truth better than the truth tracks its own re-measurement.

\subsection{Summary of correlations}
\label{app:sur-results}

Tables~\ref{tab:sur-corr-catalogue} and~\ref{tab:sur-corr-snr} give the correlation of every surrogate with the three truths over the benchmark tasks.
Four readings hold across them.
First, no surrogate reaches the ceiling. The best proxy-only statistics recover $\rho \approx 0.82$--$0.84$ of a DA-size whose own re-measurement correlates at 0.92.
Second, the statistics that track decision accuracy best describe whether the proxy's ranking and curves are stable (sign persistence, autocorrelation, monotonicity, Cronbach's $\alpha$), not how far apart the variants are. \checknum{The dispersion signals alone (range, standard deviation, pairwise distance) correlate negatively with every truth}.
Third, the SNR of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} as released reaches $\rho = 0.40$ with DA-size, against $R = 0.79$ in their setting. \checknum{Replacing the checkpoint noise by the $k$-fold benchmark noise raises every signal}.
Fourth, the benchmark noise is largely a function of the item count, and the item count alone correlates at 0.38, \checknum{as much as the best ratio}. \texttt{dispersion\_shifted} is constant, so its ratios are the inverse noise.
