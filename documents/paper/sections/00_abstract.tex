\begin{abstract}
Training multilingual language models requires repeated decisions about data mixtures and architectures, typically made on small proxy models and guided by expensive benchmark suites that exhibit high variance, particularly outside English. We ask which model sizes, training checkpoints and measurements can stand in for a large multilingual model when such a decision must be made, and how the answer depends on the number of languages. We pretrain a ladder of 186 models at six sizes from 90M to 1.7B non-embedding parameters and seven language settings from 1 to 100 languages, with four design interventions and seed replicates, and score every checkpoint on per-language bits per byte in 100 languages and on 14 multilingual benchmark families. We find that likelihood-based measurements scale predictably while four-option knowledge benchmarks sit at chance up to 1.7B; that a 350M proxy at 20\% of its run already reads a data decision the way the 1.7B reference does on bits per byte, while the benchmark suite reads every decision as a coin flip; that the reliability of a proxy is set by the size of the intervention's effect relative to seed noise rather than by the intervention itself; and that scaling exponents transfer across languages, so that one small measurement predicts a never-trained language's bits per byte at 1.7B within 7\%. We release the ladder and the per-checkpoint measurements to support cheaper, better-informed multilingual model development.
\end{abstract}
