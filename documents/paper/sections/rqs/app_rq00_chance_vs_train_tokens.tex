\subsection{Above chance against the training tokens of the language}
\label{app:rq00_chance_vs_train_tokens}

\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\paragraph{Research Question.} \emph{Does a benchmark task clear chance once the model has seen enough tokens of the task's language, and is it the exposure or the model size that decides?}

\paragraph{Setup.} We measure the share of cells above chance. A cell is one (task, size, language setting K), read at one of the ten evaluated tenths of the run. We use the seed-1904 runs of every data build and ladder, 90M--1.7B, at K $\in$ \{1, 2, 8, 15, 30, 50\}. The tasks are the benchmark tasks with a chance level in trained languages. BPB and the bBPB twins have no chance level and are left out. A cell is above chance when it passes the gate (the per-run Wilson test and the half-of-the-runs rule), applied at that tenth. A cell sits at the number of tokens of the task's language that the checkpoint had seen: the language's share of the build's mixture $\times$ the size's token budget $\times$ the tenth. Per benchmark, answer format and model size, we fit a logistic curve of the cells' above-chance verdict on the log of the language's tokens, on the non-English cells, and read the token count at which it reaches 50 \% and 90 \% of the cells (Table~\ref{tab:rq0_thresholds}). We then test whether leaving out the cells below that threshold changes how well an aggregate score ranks the designs (Table~\ref{tab:app_rq00_token_exclusion}).
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\begin{rqfinding}
\textbf{1. Outside English, the share of cells above chance rises with every decade of the language's tokens from $10^{7}$ on.} But at matched tokens the larger model still clears chance more often. The share goes from 42 \% at $10^{7}$--$10^{8}$ tokens to 64 \% at $10^{10}$--$10^{11}$. At $10^{8}$--$10^{9}$ tokens it is 43 \% at 90M against 62 \% at 1.7B, so exposure is necessary but not sufficient. (Figure~\ref{fig:app_rq00_chance_vs_train_tokens}.)
\\
\\
\textbf{2. Pooled over every language, the curve peaks at $10^{8}$--$10^{9}$ tokens and falls after, because the top bins are mostly English.} It reads 52 \% at $10^{8}$--$10^{9}$, dips to 51 \% at $10^{9}$--$10^{10}$ and drops to 41 \% at $10^{10}$--$10^{11}$ (25,217 cells, 80 \% of them English).
\\
\\
\textbf{3. Without English, the share stays near 40 \% up to $10^{8}$ tokens and rises with every decade after.} Per decade: 43 \% ($10^{5}$, 82 cells), 42 \% ($10^{6}$), 42 \% ($10^{7}$), 52 \% ($10^{8}$), 62 \% ($10^{9}$), 64 \% ($10^{10}$, 4,945 cells).
\end{rqfinding}
\end{minipage}
