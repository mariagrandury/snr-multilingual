\subsection{Design decisions}
\label{app:rq05_design_decisions}

\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\paragraph{Research Question.} \emph{At a given number of languages K, when does a small model rank a design choice (depth, data scheme, temperature) the way the 1.7B reference does, and how does the answer move with K?}

\paragraph{Setup.} We study four single-axis interventions against the baseline (deep, scheme A, $T = 1$). They are depth (deep vs shallow at equal non-embedding size), data scheme A vs B, data scheme A vs C, and sampling temperature $T = 1$ vs $T = 3$. We use every seed. We report the DA-size of the proxies 90M--1B and the DA-ckpt of the earlier checkpoints of the 1.7B run, both against the 1.7B final checkpoint. We read them on the bits per byte of the languages that both levels train and on the benchmarks above chance.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\begin{rqfinding}
\textbf{1. Per-language BPB reads the temperature and list decisions at every proxy size, the gated benchmarks almost never.} The trained languages' BPB reads temperature at 0.95--0.99 (mean over K15--K50) and the language lists at 0.77--1.00 (K8--K30), while on the benchmarks every intervention sits at 0.45--0.63 and only the two K1 English-data swaps come near 0.75 (DCLMP 0.51--0.79, 0.79 at 350M, FWEB 0.57--0.75, 0.746 at 600M). (Figure~\ref{fig:app_rq05_design_decisions}.)
\\
\\
\textbf{2. Per-language BPB reads temperature and the language lists in almost every cell.} The temperature decision is read at 0.87--1.00 by every proxy at every K, and the language-list decision at 0.83--1.00 except 1B at K30 (0.31).
\\
\\
\textbf{3. The depth decision on per-language BPB collapses at 350M and 600M.} It is 0.00 for 600M at all four K (the 600M depth pairs are not size-matched) and 0.00--0.97 for 350M, while 90M, 175M and 1B read it at 0.78--1.00.
\end{rqfinding}
\end{minipage}
