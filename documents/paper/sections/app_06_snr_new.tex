\section{Surrogates of Decision Accuracy}
\label{app:surrogates}
\label{subsec:methodology-snr}

Decision accuracy (DA) needs the reference: it compares the ranking of the proxies with the ranking of the large models. A \emph{surrogate} is a statistic computed on the proxy alone that aims to predict this DA, so that a developer can tell whether the proxy's ranking can be trusted without training the reference. This appendix defines every surrogate we score (\S\ref{app:sur-snr}--\ref{app:sur-catalogue}), how we score them (\S\ref{app:sur-scoring}) and the results (\S\ref{app:sur-results}).

\subsection{Truths and scoring}
\label{app:sur-scoring}
\label{app:sur-notation}

A task is scored on the $V$ design variants of one proxy size. Each variant has a final score and a training curve evaluated at ten tenths of its run. Its \emph{noise window} is its scores at 80, 85, 90, 95 and 100\% of the run.

We score each surrogate against three DA readings, which we call \emph{truths} (Appendix~\ref{app:ranking-agreement}): DA-size, the final ranking of the proxy against the final ranking of the reference; DA-goal, an intermediate checkpoint of the proxy against the final ranking of the reference; and DA-ckpt, an intermediate checkpoint of the proxy against its own final ranking. A cell is a (task, proxy size, fraction of the run), and a truth is read only on cells above chance with at least three pairs.

For each surrogate and truth, we report Spearman's $\rho$ over one point per (benchmark, language) cluster: the mean surrogate of the cluster against its mean truth, pooled over proxy sizes and checkpoints. A task and its reformulated versions form one cluster. The Benjamini--Hochberg correction runs over every configuration we search. As a \emph{ceiling}, we correlate each truth with itself when the reference is read at 90\% of its run instead of 100\%: no surrogate can be expected to track a truth better than the truth tracks its own re-measurement.

\subsection{Signal-to-noise ratios}
\label{app:sur-snr}
\label{app:sur-signals}
\label{app:sur-noise}

\citet{heineman_signal_2025} define a benchmark's signal-to-noise ratio as the spread of the final scores of models of similar compute (signal) divided by the variability of one model's score across its last checkpoints (noise). Their headline definition is the relative dispersion over the relative checkpoint noise, which they find to correlate with DA at $R = 0.79$. We compute every signal and noise of their released code and score all $22 \times 6 = 132$ ratios, as well as each signal and each noise alone.

\textbf{Signals.} Table~\ref{tab:sur-signals} defines the 22 signals. With $c_1, \dots, c_V$ the final scores of the variants and $\bar{c}$ their mean, most of them measure the spread of the $c_i$, either in score units or divided by the mean score (the ``relative'' signals). A few treat the scores as points in $[0, 1]$ and measure how evenly they cover it, and two measure how central each variant is among the others.

\textbf{Noises.} Six noises enter the grid.
\emph{Checkpoint noise} is the standard deviation of a variant's score over its noise window, averaged over variants. We remove the linear trend of the window before taking it, because scores still rise during the learning-rate decay and the raw deviation would count that rise as noise. \texttt{ckpt\_abs} is this noise in score units, and \texttt{ckpt\_rel} divides it by the mean score, as in the released code.
\emph{Benchmark noise} is the standard deviation of a variant's score across $k = 5$ disjoint folds of the test items, averaged over variants. It needs a single evaluation run and no intermediate checkpoint. We use its expected value over random folds, $\sqrt{p(1-p)(k-1)/(n-1)}$ for accuracy $p$ on $n$ items, and the same with the variance of the item scores for the bBPB twins. \texttt{kfold\_abs} is this noise in score units, and \texttt{kfold\_rel} divides it by the score.
The two \emph{depth noises} pair with the depth signals: the checkpoint noise divided by the mean depth of the window scores.
\citet{heineman_signal_2025} also define seed noise, data-order noise and total variation, which they use only to validate the checkpoint noise. We use the seed noise to size effects, not as a surrogate.

\subsection{Other surrogates}
\label{app:sur-finetasks}
\label{app:sur-catalogue}

\textbf{FineTasks criteria.} FineTasks \citep{kydlicek_finetasks_nodate, penedo_fineweb2_2025} keeps a task when four statistics computed along training pass a threshold: \emph{monotonicity}, the mean Spearman correlation between training step and score; \emph{low noise}, the mean final score divided by the mean spread across runs; \emph{non-randomness}, the best final score above the random baseline in units of the spread; and \emph{ordering consistency}, the mean Kendall's $\tau$ between the run rankings at consecutive steps.

\textbf{Catalogue from the literature.} Table~\ref{tab:sur-catalogue} defines the remaining statistics, grouped by the question they ask. Most were proposed for another unit of replication, such as test items, topics or seeds, and we read each on what one proxy size offers: its noise window, its training curve and the smaller rungs. Throughout, a pair's gap is measured in units of the two variants' checkpoint noise.

We cannot compute statistics that need per-item log-probabilities, such as item response models or paired significance tests, because the ladder report holds one aggregate score per checkpoint.

\subsection{Results}
\label{app:sur-results}

Tables~\ref{tab:sur-corr-catalogue} and~\ref{tab:sur-corr-snr} give the correlation of every surrogate with the three truths, and Table~\ref{tab:sur-corr-strata} gives the correlation with DA-size within single strata. Three findings stand out.
First, no surrogate reaches the ceiling: DA-size correlates with its own re-measurement at 0.90, and the best proxy-only statistics reach 0.81.
Second, the statistics that track DA best describe whether the proxy's ranking holds still, such as the proxy's own DA-ckpt and sign persistence (both 0.81), or agrees with the rung below (0.77). The spread of the final scores alone carries almost no information ($\rho$ between $-0.04$ and $0.07$ for \texttt{range} and \texttt{rel\_std} against DA-size). It becomes informative only once divided by the checkpoint noise: the relative-standard-deviation SNR reaches 0.75 and the headline SNR of \citet{heineman_signal_2025} 0.65, against $R = 0.79$ in their setting, while dividing by the $k$-fold benchmark noise instead gives 0.21.
Third, the pooled correlations hide large differences between strata. Within one proxy size the relative-standard-deviation SNR reaches only 0.60--0.62, within HellaSwag 0.80 and on the per-language bits per byte 0.46.
