\begin{abstract}
Design choices for training large multilingual language models are typically guided by results from ablations done using smaller, proxy models. However, such results are only useful if evaluations of small-scale models reliably predict an ablation's effects in larger-scale models. The reliability of these evaluations has not been well-studied in the multilingual domain. 
Here we provide a characterization of when small-scale models can reliably guide larger model development. 
We pretrain a controlled ladder of \nmodels multilingual models, spanning scales and language mixtures, %spanning six sizes from 90M to 1.7B non-embedding parameters and six language settings from 1 to 50 languages, with four design interventions and seed replicates, providing a testbed for the reliability of evaluations as effective predictors of scaling behavior. 
and evaluate language modelling and downstream performance on \nbenchmarks multilingual benchmark families. %Our results show that benchmarks commonly used for multilingual pretraining ablations are at chance at small scales and do not scale reliably.
