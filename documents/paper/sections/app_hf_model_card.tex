\subsection*{Model Card for the Multilingual Signal-and-Noise Model Ladder}

This card describes a ladder of \nruns decoder-only language models with 90M to 3B non-embedding parameters. We pretrained them from scratch on controlled mixtures of English and up to 49 other languages, and we release every saved checkpoint of every run. We built the ladder to study which benchmarks give a reliable signal when small models guide pretraining decisions.

\subsection*{Model Details}

\subsubsection*{Model Description}

The ladder keeps one training recipe and changes one design decision at a time. Each run sets the following factors.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Model size $N$:} 90M, 175M, 350M, 600M, 1B, 1.7B or 3B non-embedding parameters. The 3B models are deep models trained with seed 1904, under scheme A at $K \in \{8, 15, 30, 50\}$ and under scheme B at $K \in \{8, 15\}$.
    \item \textbf{Number of languages $K$:} 1, 2, 8, 15, 30 or 50. A model trains on English and $K-1$ other languages. At $K=1$ it trains on English only.
    \item \textbf{Data scheme:} the rule that chooses the data. Scheme A takes the largest FineWeb-2 languages. Scheme B chooses languages for script and family diversity at $K \in \{8, 15, 30\}$. It also replaces Russian with Chinese at $K=2$, and the English corpus with DCLM before its educational filter at $K=1$. Scheme C uses Spanish at $K=2$ and FineWeb as the English corpus at $K=1$.
    \item \textbf{Sampling temperature $T$:} 1, which samples each language in proportion to its data, or 3, which flattens the shares. $T=3$ uses the scheme-A lists at $K \in \{15, 30, 50\}$.
    \item \textbf{Depth:} deep or shallow. A shallow model has about twice the width-to-depth ratio of the deep model of the same size.
    \item \textbf{Activation:} xIELU or SwiGLU. The SwiGLU models are deep models under scheme A at $K \in \{8, 15, 30\}$.
    \item \textbf{Optimizer:} AdEMAMix or Muon. The Muon models cover the same sizes and settings as the SwiGLU models.
    \item \textbf{Seed:} 1904 for every configuration. A few deep configurations add two seeds, which change both the initialization and the data order. Seeds 64 and 313 train at 175M and 600M for $K \in \{1, 2, 50\}$. Seeds 28 and 1797 train at 1B for $K \in \{1, 2, 30\}$, and also for scheme B at $K=30$.
\end{itemize}

Every model trains on $D = 100N$ tokens, five times the Chinchilla budget of $20N$. In every mixture with $K \ge 2$, half of the tokens are English. The section on Training Details lists the sizes, the data builds and the languages.

\subsection*{Uses}

\subsubsection*{Direct Use}

The models are made for research on pretraining and evaluation. Examples are ablations of data mixtures and architectures at small scale, scaling-law fits over model size and training time, and studies of benchmark signal and noise across checkpoints. The models can also serve as reference proxies when one chooses benchmarks for a multilingual pretraining run.

\subsubsection*{Downstream Use}

The ladder gives many comparable models trained on documented data. They can be starting points for continued pretraining or fine-tuning experiments. They do not follow instructions.

\subsubsection*{Out-of-Scope Use}

The models are not meant for user-facing applications, for generating factual content or for any high-stakes setting. They are small and have no instruction or safety tuning. Their outputs are unreliable and can be harmful.

\subsection*{Bias, Risks, and Limitations}

\textbf{Data.} The models inherit the biases of web text and of its language distribution. English is half of every multilingual mixture, and the other languages follow their share of the available data. At $K=50$ and $T=1$, Russian receives 19.2\% of the non-English half and Malayalam 0.09\%. The non-English data was also not filtered alike for every language. A quality classifier selected about one document in ten for 20 of the 49 languages, while a random sample of the same rate replaced it for the other 29. Only 8 languages went through a toxicity filter. A comparison between languages therefore mixes the language with the quality of its data.

\textbf{Repetition and data order.} Some settings repeat data. At $K=2$, the 1.7B model sees its Russian 1.15 times, its Chinese 1.61 times and its Spanish 3.51 times. Three settings (scheme A at $K \in \{15, 50\}$ and scheme B at $K=15$) give the 1.7B model a larger build than the smaller models. It continues each language with documents from later crawls. The 3B models read their own, larger builds.

\textbf{Training.} The training documents are concatenated without separator tokens. The models never saw a beginning-of-sequence or end-of-sequence token, so they do not stop generating on their own. The 90M and 175M models train at a smaller batch (84 and 168 sequences), because their runs at 504 sequences diverged. A shallow model differs in size from its deep counterpart by 0.4\% to 5.2\%. Most configurations train a single seed.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Many multilingual benchmark tasks stay at chance level at these sizes. For example, most Belebele and Global-MMLU tasks stay at chance up to 1.7B in their original multiple-choice format. English bits-per-byte is measured on held-out DCLM-Edu text, which favors the DCLM-Edu models over the other two English corpora at $K=1$.

\subsubsection*{Recommendations}

Compare models only within the controlled settings of the ladder. Before reading a benchmark at a given size, check that its scores are above chance. Use the replicate seeds as the noise floor of a ranking. Report the epoch counts whenever you compare the three $K=2$ builds, and do not use English bits-per-byte to rank the three $K=1$ English corpora. Do not use any checkpoint as an assistant.

\subsection*{How to Get Started with the Model}

Each run is one repository, named after its size, $K$, data build, ladder and seed. Each saved checkpoint is a branch named \texttt{step-} followed by the zero-padded six-digit step, and \texttt{main} holds the final checkpoint. The deep, shallow and Muon models use the Apertus architecture of Hugging Face Transformers. The SwiGLU models use the Qwen3 architecture, because the Apertus MLP has no gate. Each repository includes the tokenizer, and the weights are stored in bf16.

\begin{small}
\begin{verbatim}
from transformers import (
    AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer)

repo = "<org>/<run>"  # removed for anonymity
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(repo)
# the final checkpoint
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
    repo)
# the middle of a deep 1.7B run
mid = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
    repo, revision="step-040500")
\end{verbatim}
\end{small}

\subsection*{Training Details}

\subsubsection*{Training Data}

English comes from DCLM-Edu \citep{allal_smollm2_2025}, the part of DCLM \citep{li_datacomp-lm_2025} that an educational-quality classifier \citep{lozhkov_fineweb-edu_2024} keeps. The other languages come from a quality-filtered copy of FineWeb-2 \citep{penedo_fineweb2_2025,messmer_enhancing_2026}. Every multilingual model blends the two halves 50/50 at training time. At $K=1$, two alternative builds replace DCLM-Edu with the DCLM pool before the educational filter (DCLMP) or with FineWeb restricted to the crawls up to 2022 (FWEB) \citep{penedo_fineweb_2024}. One validation set covers English and 99 FineWeb-2 languages, and every training build excludes it.

\subsubsection*{Training Procedure}

\paragraph{Preprocessing}

We tokenize every document with the 131,072-token Apertus tokenizer, without special tokens. Megatron-LM shuffles the documents with the seed of the run, concatenates them without a separator and cuts the stream into sequences of 4,096 tokens.

\paragraph{Training Hyperparameters}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Training regime:} bf16 mixed precision with fp32 gradient accumulation, data parallelism only
    \item \textbf{Optimizer:} AdEMAMix \citep{pagliardini_ademamix_2024} with $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$, $\beta_3 = 0.9999$ and $\alpha = 8$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta_3$ warm up over the whole run. Weight decay is 0.1 and gradients are clipped at norm 0.1.
    \item \textbf{Muon ladder:} Muon \citep{jordan_muon_2024} with the Moonlight recipe \citep{liu_muon_2025} on the hidden weight matrices, with momentum 0.95, Nesterov updates and five Newton-Schulz steps. Each update of an $m \times n$ matrix is scaled by $0.2\sqrt{\max(m, n)}$, so Muon keeps the peak learning rate and weight decay of the deep ladder. AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$) trains the embeddings, the output layer and the norms.
    \item \textbf{Schedule:} warmup-stable-decay \citep{hagele_scaling_2024}, with a linear warmup over about 4\% of the steps and a $1-\sqrt{\cdot}$ decay to zero over the last 20\%
    \item \textbf{Peak learning rate:} $\eta = 0.3118\, C^{-1/8}$ at the budget of the run, with $C = 6ND = 600N^2$
    \item \textbf{Initialization:} standard deviation $0.008944\sqrt{1792/d_{\mathrm{model}}}$
    \item \textbf{Batch:} 504 sequences of 4,096 tokens (about 2.06M tokens) from 350M up, 84 sequences at 90M and 168 at 175M
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Speeds, Sizes, Times}

Each run saves 20 evenly spaced checkpoints, 40 at 1B and 60 at 1.7B and 3B. Sixteen 1B runs were trained before this rule and save 20, every 2,287 steps up to step 45,740. Pretraining the ladder used \npretrainnodehours node-hours on nodes of four GPUs.

\subsection*{Evaluation}

\subsubsection*{Testing Data, Factors \& Metrics}

\paragraph{Testing Data}

We evaluate twelve checkpoints of each run, at every tenth of training and at 85\% and 95\%. Bits-per-byte uses the held-out validation set of English and 99 FineWeb-2 languages, about 5M tokens per language, of which we score the first 1M. The benchmarks are \nbenchanalysed benchmarks (\ntasksanalysed benchmark-language tasks) in the LM Evaluation Harness \citep{gao_framework_2021}, scored zero-shot.

\paragraph{Factors}

Model size, number of languages, data scheme, temperature, depth, activation, optimizer, seed and training checkpoint.

\paragraph{Metrics}

Accuracy or normalized accuracy per benchmark-language task, read against its chance level. A model counts as above chance when the lower bound of the one-sided 95\% Wilson interval of its accuracy exceeds chance. Bits-per-byte per language. Across the ladder, decision accuracy measures how often a smaller model ranks two training choices the same way as the 1.7B reference, and the signal-to-noise ratio compares the spread of scores across models with their spread across late checkpoints.

\subsection*{Technical Specifications}

\subsubsection*{Model Architecture and Objective}

Decoder-only transformer trained with next-token prediction. It uses grouped-query attention with four query heads per key-value head and head dimension 64, rotary position embeddings with base 500,000 and Llama-3 scaling factor 8, RMSNorm and QK-norm. The MLP uses xIELU \citep{huang_xielu_2025}, or SwiGLU in the SwiGLU ladder. There are no bias terms and no dropout. Input and output embeddings are tied over the 131,072-token vocabulary, and the context length is 4,096 tokens.

\subsubsection*{Compute Infrastructure}

\paragraph{Hardware}

GH200 nodes with four GPUs each.

\paragraph{Software}

Megatron-LM \citep{shoeybi_megatron-lm_2019} for training, Hugging Face Transformers for the released checkpoints, and the LM Evaluation Harness with vLLM in bf16 for evaluation.
