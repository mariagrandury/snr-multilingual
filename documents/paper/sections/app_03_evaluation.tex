\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{app:evaluation-protocol}

\textbf{Bits-per-byte implementation.} We measure the negative log-likelihood of held-out text in bits, normalized by its UTF-8 byte count.
For language $\ell$, let $x_1,\ldots,x_{m_\ell}$ be the evaluated token sequence, $c_t$ the context preceding token $x_t$ within the scoring block, and $b_\ell$ the number of bytes in the evaluated text. We compute
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BPB}_\ell = \frac{-\sum_{t=2}^{m_\ell}\log_2 p(x_t \mid c_t)}{b_\ell}.
\label{eq:bpb}
\end{equation}

We concatenate validation documents without end-of-document separators. We score them in blocks of 4,096 input tokens, with a one-token overlap between successive blocks. Each token except the first of the stream is scored once, and the context resets at block boundaries. We select a deterministic prefix that ends at the document boundary that reaches 1M tokens, or the full stream if it is shorter. We measure the denominator by decoding the entire selected prefix and counting its UTF-8 bytes, including the initial conditioning token. Thus, the implementation omits the likelihood of the first token but keeps its bytes. We also compute token-level perplexity from the same forward passes.

\textbf{Task selection.} We select the registered tasks whose language is in the training mixture. Task counts can exceed family counts. For the resource-ranked lists, the number of tasks grows with $K$: \ntasksKone at $K=1$, \ntasksKtwo at $K=2$, \ntasksKeight at $K=8$, \ntasksKfifteen at $K=15$, \ntasksKthirty at $K=30$, and \ntasksKfifty at $K=50$. These counts cover the whole evaluated group of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-families}, that is, the multilingual families, the reformulated variants of the letter-format families (\S\ref{subsec:rq1}) and the English-text benchmarks. Every trained language is covered by at least one family. For the seed-1904 deep cells of scheme A at every $K$ and of scheme B at $K=8$, we also run every task in every language, independent of the training mixture. This lets us read cross-lingual transfer on the benchmarks as well as on bits-per-byte. 

\textbf{Language coverage.} Every trained language is covered by at least one benchmark family. English is covered by 14 of the multilingual families plus 11 of the English-text benchmarks. Of the 100 languages of Table~\ref{tab:app-languages}, eighteen have exactly one family and fourteen have two. The languages with a single family are covered by Belebele (12), MultiBLiMP (4) or Global PIQA (2) alone. For those languages, the per-language benchmark read rests on one task. Counting tasks instead of families, a cell is evaluated on \ntasksKone tasks at $K=1$, \ntasksKtwo at $K=2$, \ntasksKeight at $K=8$, \ntasksKfifteen at $K=15$, \ntasksKthirty at $K=30$ and \ntasksKfifty at $K=50$, including the reformulated variants and the English-only benchmarks. Two IrokoBench families (AfriXNLI and AfriMMLU) are wired but disabled because of a loading defect in the pinned harness. Enabling them again would add a second or third human-translated family for Amharic, Igbo, Kinyarwanda, Southern Sotho, Xhosa and Zulu.


\textbf{Harness settings.} All benchmarks run through a fork of the LM Evaluation Harness \citep{gao_framework_2021}. We install it from a pinned shared checkout, and each evaluation job records the source commit. We use the vLLM backend \citep{kwon_efficient_2023}, a maximum context of 4,096 tokens, a beginning-of-sequence token before every prompt, no chat template, and the default prompt and number of in-context examples of each task. We report accuracy for multiple-choice and completion tasks and exact match for the generative ones. The exception is the tasks whose options are answer strings scored as continuations, that is, the cloze-format originals (ARC, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, MathQA and Global PIQA) and the reformulated variants, which we score with length-normalised accuracy. We aggregate sub-tasks (for example the MMLU subjects) to their family with the harness's own grouping. Base models at these sizes are at chance on several families. The above-chance threshold of Appendix~\ref{app:chance-threshold} decides, per task and size, whether such a cell enters the analysis.
