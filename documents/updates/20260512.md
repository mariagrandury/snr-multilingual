# Results 12/05/2026

## SNR variant choice and cross-corpus transfer

**Training curves are clean.** 36 Apertus pretrains × ~13 ckpts each, mean-pooled across seeds, give monotone acc-vs-FLOPs scaling on high-signal families. ([acc_vs_flops/seeds_28_1797_1904/](../src/signal-and-noise/results/acc_vs_flops/seeds_28_1797_1904/))

**Dispersion family is the portable default.** Of 22 SNR aggregator variants, the dispersion cluster (`quartile_deviation`, `aad`, `mpd`, `rms_deviation`, `dist_std`, `range`) ties within ~0.02 at mean Pearson r ≈ **0.38** vs decision accuracy across 12 languages on the pooled `{28, 1797, 1904}` corpus. Exact per-language picks do _not_ generalize across seed splits (1/14 match) but the global ranking does (Spearman ρ = **+0.84** DA-size, **+0.90** DA-ckpt). Treat per-language tuning as overfitting.

**Cross-corpus transfer.** `log10(SNR_1B)` correlates Apertus ↔ AllenAI DataDecide at Pearson r = **0.935** for `star_discrepancy_shifted` (7 shared English tasks). The discrepancy family transfers cleanly; the relative-spread family (incl. upstream `rel_std`) does _not_. Top-5 agreement is **5/5** in both corpora.

## What makes a multilingual benchmark reliable

**Task design predicts SNR, curation doesn't.** Per-family SNR ranges from `multiblimp` (3.81) to `arc` (0.62). Translation method (MT vs human vs template) has _no_ significant effect (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.66). The only significant single-axis predictor is **n_options** (Spearman ρ = **+0.62**, p = 0.04): 2-option tasks beat 4-option. Option length is borderline (ρ = +0.54). Belebele loses on both axes (4-opt MRC, short labels); MultiBLiMP wins on both.

**Subset selection ~doubles SNR on the low-tier.** Sorting subtasks by standalone SNR and walking a cumulative prefix sweep, **belebele** jumps 2.28 → 3.44 (+1.16, 3-language subset); **xcopa** 2.33 → 3.30 (+0.97, `xcopa_eu` alone); **GMF subjects** 2.12 → 3.65 (+1.52, `medical_genetics` at 175M). High-SNR families (multiblimp, xstorycloze, hellaswag) saturate above ~2.5 and no subset helps further.

**Top-5 coverage flips the ranking.** Counting how often a family appears in _any_ language's top-5 SNR benchmarks (across all 12 languages, pooled 9 model_families per size), **belebele** leads at **9/12** languages — despite its low full-set SNR — because it has wide language coverage and its per-language tasks each clear the bar. Multiblimp drops to 5/12 (limited language inventory).

## Key Takeaways

- **Dispersion family ≈ portable SNR default**; per-language tuning overfits across seeds
- **Cross-corpus transfer holds** (r = 0.93 vs AllenAI; top-5 reliable benchmarks agree 5/5)
- **Task design > curation** for benchmark reliability (n_options, option length)
- **Subset selection lifts low-tier families** by ~1 SNR point; high-tier already saturated
- **Belebele covers 9/12 languages' top-5** despite low full-set SNR — wide-coverage benchmarks deserve their own metric
