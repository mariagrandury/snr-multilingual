1: # RQ1 — What scales predictably? (paper RQ1)
2: 
3: ## Research question
4: 
5: > Which benchmarks, and which per-language measurements, move with model size
6: > in a way a log-linear fit captures, so that a small-model measurement has a
7: > trend to extrapolate from at all? The paper's RQ1
8: > ([`documents/paper/sections/04_analysis.tex`](../../../../documents/paper/sections/04_analysis.tex)).
9: > The folder also holds the loss scaling fit and, in `scaling_law_error.py`,
10: > the prediction test: how well a power law fitted on the proxy rungs
11: > predicts the reference rung's per-language BPB.
12: 
20: 
21: ## Setup
22: 
23: Pool `predictivity_all` (every seed and scheme; the fits use the deep,
24: scheme-A, seed-1904 cells), sizes 90M–1.7B, the rq00 gate per (task, size)
25: (`predictivity` mask), trained languages and parent tasks only; no design
26: pairs are involved, so no pair set. Hand-written numbers are from the
27: ladder-report snapshot **2026-09-30 23:54**.
28: 
29: ## Experimental setup
30: 
31: The fits read the plan grid — deep, scheme A, seed 1904 — at each cell's
32: final checkpoint, from 90M up (each rung at its own batch since the
33: 2026-09-23 retrain; the diverged batch-504 90M/175M runs are dropped at load,
34: [`plan/90M-rung-anomaly.md`](../../../../plan/90M-rung-anomaly.md)); the
35: loader keeps parent tasks and trained languages only
36: ([`RULES.md`](../RULES.md), rules 6 and 2). Every task is one series per
37: language setting L; per-language BPB and the training loss enter as tasks too
38: (they fall with size, so ρ = −1 is their ideal). The above-random gate (rule 1)
39: applies per (task, size): a rung enters a task's fit only where rq00's mask
40: says the task is above chance there; tasks without a chance level (BPB, the
41: loss, generative tasks) are never gated. The scaling-law error uses the deep,
42: scheme-A, seed-1904 cells at every L on the languages the cell trains, with
43: the 1.7B rung as the only reference (rule 9); the loss scaling fit the
44: seed-1904 cell of every (L, arch, scheme) the pool holds.
45: 
46: ## Methodology
47: 
48: - **Log-N fit.** Per (task, L): score = a + b·log₁₀ N over the rungs where
49:   the task is above chance (≥ 3 of them; `n_rungs` and `gated_rungs` in the
50:   table, and `gated` where the gate left fewer than 3 — the row stays, with
51:   NaN statistics, and is grey in the panels), with R² and the Spearman ρ
52:   between score and N; medians per family over its fits (≥ 3), the family's
53:   modal option count alongside (`rq1_fits.csv`, `rq1_families.csv`).
54: - **Loss scaling fit.** Final training loss of the seed-1904 cells against N
55:   per (L, arch, scheme), `pretrain.ladder_report._fit` over every rung present
56:   (the count per fit is in the legend and the README bullet) — the health
57:   check's law, but with every rung in the fit rather than the smallest
58:   predicted.
59: - **Scaling-law error.** Per (L, arch, scheme, language), log BPB = a − α log N
60:   is fitted on the proxy rungs up to a ladder top (≥ 3 points, the same
61:   `_fit`) and predicts the 1.7B BPB; a chain without a 1.7B final is dropped
62:   and named, never referenced at a smaller size. The relative error is signed
63:   ((predicted − observed) / observed, negative = the law under-predicts BPB)
64:   and reported per ladder top, so the table reads "how far up the ladder must
65:   one train before the reference is predicted within x %". A constant offset
66:   between small and large models shows up here but not in decision accuracy
67:   (rq05), so the two reads can disagree.

95: 
96: - Scaling is predictable on the tasks that survive the gate: of the 346 tasks
97:   with a regime, 266 are predictable across both size and training, 50
98:   across size only, 24 weak in both, 1 predictable during training only and

103: - The fit is made on the rungs that already cleared chance — all 127
104:   three-rung fits lost their smallest rungs to the gate — so a line
105:   through the rising top of a sigmoid is fitted and R² is inflated by
106:   construction; and the reference is inside every fit, so R² is a property of
107:   the ladder including 1.7B, not a statement about predicting 1.7B from
108:   below (figure 3 is that statement).
109: - With and without the twins: the 346 tasks are 203 originals (median
110:   size-fit R² 0.911) and 143 twins (0.855), with the same regime shares to
111:   within a few points, so the twins change the population, not the verdict
112:   ([rq00 figure 3](../rq00_gate_and_curves/README.md#3-the-reformulated-twins-move-whole-families-across-the-gate)).
113: - Spearman ρ over six points saturates less than over five (ρ = 1 in 25 %
114:   of the 2 481 benchmark fits now that the 90M rung is in; 67 % of the
115:   1 044 five-rung fits of the 09-23 snapshot), and the trajectory fit runs
116:   over all saved points under a WSD decay
117:   that is not log-linear in tokens, so part of panel (b)'s spread is

181: ### 3. Scaling-law error: predicting the 1.7B BPB from below
182: 
183: *`predictivity_all`, deep scheme-A seed-1904 cells, per-language BPB
184: (ungated: no chance level), a power law fitted on the proxy rungs up to a
185: ladder top and evaluated at the 1.7B reference (rule 11: the reference never
186: enters the fit).* The leave-top-out error is the prediction test figure 1's
187: R² cannot be.
188: 
211: 
212: **Key findings**
213: 
214: - The 1.7B BPB is under-predicted from every ladder top: median absolute
215:   relative error 11.5 % with three rungs (175M–600M) and 5.7 % with four
216:   (175M–1B), and every fit over-predicts the improvement (the signed error
217:   is negative throughout). The exponent fitted on the small rungs is too
218:   steep for the reference's regime — a curvature finding, not a symmetric
219:   error bar.
220: - A constant offset between small and large models shows up here but not in
221:   decision accuracy (rq02, rq05), so the two reads can disagree.
222:

323: ### 5. Scaling cleanly and ranking like the reference are different properties
324: 
325: *Per task: figure 1's ρ (score with model size along the ladder, `rho_size`)
326: against rq02's ρ (the proxy's ranking of the design variants with the 1.7B
327: ranking, `agreement_da_size_per_cell_multi_axes.csv`; DA-size, multi-axis pairs from
328: `predictivity_schemes` at seed 1904, gate `predictivity`). The script and its
329: outputs live in rq02's folder
330: (`../rq02_decision_accuracy/scaling_vs_ranking.py`; auto block in
331: [rq02's README](../rq02_decision_accuracy/README.md#11-read-next-in-the-other-rqs)).*
332: 
333: ![Scaling against ranking](../rq02_decision_accuracy/pretraining/predictivity/scaling_vs_ranking_da_size_multi_axes.png)
334: 
335: *One point per task with both a scaling regime (rq01) and a decision-accuracy
336: cell at the proxy (rq02). Top: rq01's ρ (jittered, it lives on a lattice)
337: against rq02's ρ. Bottom: rq01's R² against DA-size. The corner number is
338: the Spearman correlation across tasks.*
339: 
340: **Key findings**
341: 
342: - A benchmark that scales cleanly ranks the variants only somewhat better:
343:   across tasks, Spearman between rq01's ρ and rq02's ρ is 0.12 at the 175M
344:   proxy, 0.23 at 350M, 0.28 at 600M and 0.37 at 1B (167–274 tasks); between
345:   rq01's R² and DA-size 0.16 → 0.34.
346: - The tasks rq01 calls "predictable across both" read DA-size 0.54–0.59 at
347:   the four proxies against 0.44–0.46 for the other regimes; the trajectory
348:   fit is the better of rq01's statistics as a surrogate (0.38 → 0.62 with
349:   DA-size), the same monotonicity property FineTasks selects on
350:   ([rq04, FineTasks' criteria](../rq04_surrogates/README.md#finetasks-criteria-on-the-ladder)).
351: - Scaling smoothly with size is necessary for ranking on this ladder (almost
352:   every task with DA-size above 0.7 sits at ρ = 1) but far from sufficient:
353:   at ρ = 1 the ranking correlation spans −0.5 to 0.9. Choosing benchmarks by
354:   their scaling curves alone keeps many tasks that decide nothing.
