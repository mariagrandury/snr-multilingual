1: # RQ5 — Which proxy sizes rank a design decision like the reference, and how does that depend on the number of languages?
2: 
3: ## Research question
4: 
5: > At a given number of languages L, when does a small model rank a design
6: > choice the way the largest model trained at that L does — and how does the
7: > answer move with L? This is the predictivity question of
8: > [`plan/small-to-large-predictivity-training-plan.md`](../../../../plan/small-to-large-predictivity-training-plan.md):
9: > rq00–rq04 ask which *benchmarks* carry reliable signal, this RQ asks which
10: > *model sizes* do — and, in `early_decision.py`, how early in the proxy's
11: > run (the paper's RQ2 and RQ4).
12: 
22: 
23: ## Experimental setup
24: 
25: The grid is the predictivity ladder: sizes 90M–1.7B (non-embedding), language
26: settings L ∈ {1, 2, 8, 15, 30, 50, 100}, two intervention axes — model depth
27: (deep, width/depth ≈ 64, vs shallow, ≈ 128, at equal non-embedding size) and
28: data scheme (A resource-ranked vs B diversity-first language sets, which differ
29: only at L ∈ {8, 15, 30}) — and seed replicates on the ×3 cells. Every read uses
30: each cell's final checkpoint (D = 100·N tokens, WSD-annealed). The reference at
31: each L is the largest size trained there (1.7B at every L since 2026-09-26); a
32: proxy is every smaller size. The diverged batch-504 90M and 175M runs (see
33: [`plan/90M-rung-anomaly.md`](../../../../plan/90M-rung-anomaly.md)) are dropped
34: at load in favour of their batch-84 / batch-168 retrains (rule 10), and runs
35: that have not reached their target are excluded by the loader.
36: 
37: Populations for the decision: per-language BPB on the languages both levels
38: train (`bpb_trained`, the plan's primary outcome), on all 100 validation
39: languages (`bpb_all`, zero-shot transfer included), the benchmark tasks the
40: cell was evaluated on (`benchmark`), and the single macro-BPB decision
41: (`bpb_macro`, the "aggregate criterion" the plan asks to compare against the
42: per-language one).
43: 
44: ## Methodology
45: 
46: - **Intervention decision accuracy.** For each item of a population, the
47:   decision is which level of the intervention is better; DA(proxy, L) is the
48:   fraction of items on which the proxy agrees with the reference
49:   With two levels the pairwise-ranking definition of Heineman et al. (2025)
50:   reduces to sign agreement on the two models of one item, which is
51:   `snr.metrics.decision_acc_fast` per item; items the reference ties are
52:   dropped rather than counted as misses, since they leave no decision to
53:   agree with (the one place this RQ departs from the kernel). `n_items` is reported with every cell; a benchmark cell
54:   needs ≥ 3 items.
55: - **Effect at the reference.** Per intervention and L, the median |Δ| at
56:   the reference in per-task seed standard deviations (the seed sd of the
57:   baseline cells) — the paper's "is there a decision to make?". The two
58:   other reads this folder used to carry live with their themes: the
59:   scaling-law error in rq01 (`scaling_law_error.py`) and the effect against
60:   seed and checkpoint noise per cell in rq03 (`effect_vs_noise.py`).

170: ## How small, and how early (paper RQ2)
171: 
172: ### Setup
173: 
174: Everything comes from the decision table above: with two levels,
175: decision accuracy is the share of population items on which the proxy
176: prefers the level the reference prefers. The reference is the largest size
177: trained at both levels, at its final checkpoint; the proxy is every smaller
178: size, read at the checkpoint nearest 1C, 2C, 3C, 4C and 5C of training (C = the
179: Chinchilla-optimal 20 tokens per parameter; 5C is the full run) — one grid answers both halves of the question, how small and how
180: early. Two populations: the per-language BPB of the languages both levels
181: train, and the benchmark tasks both levels were evaluated on. A cell needs
182: at least three items.
183: 
184: ### Methodology
185: 
186: The per-(intervention, L, population, proxy size, fraction) rows of the two
187: planned decisions are averaged over L, so that every language setting counts
188: once. `cells` in the table is the number of settings behind a mean; the
189: reference each setting resolved against is carried in `refs`.

252: ## Caveats to carry into the paper
253: 
254: - The depth intervention's effect is small by design (aspect ratio near the
255:   optimum); the effect-vs-noise table decides whether its DA is interpretable
256:   at all. The scheme intervention changes the language set, so its
257:   `bpb_trained` population is the languages both schemes train.
258: - Checkpoint noise windows differ by size unless the shared grid is used
259:   (the loader's default): 5 late checkpoints span the final 25 % of a
260:   20-checkpoint run and 12.5 % of a 40-checkpoint one
261:   ([`plan/1b-models.md`](../../../../plan/1b-models.md)).
262: - The reference is the largest size trained at both levels: 1.7B for every
263:   intervention and L since the L15 AT3, ZH and ES 1.7B cells finished
264:   (2026-09-26). The `reference_size` column names it per cell.

290: **Key findings** (`da_all_lines_mono_axis`, `da_all_lines_decided_mono_axis`, `depth_crossover`;
291: population, sizes and reference as in the setup above: `predictivity_all`,
292: the reference per (intervention, L) the largest size trained at both levels,
293: items the reference ties dropped)
294: 
295: - Most decisions on the shared languages are not decisions at the reference:
296:   the effect table above puts depth at 1.3 seed sds on BPB (median over L,
297:   1.15–1.34) and the language lists at 1.6 (0.6–2.9), against temperature
298:   at 5.4 (4.4–7.5); on the benchmarks every intervention sits at 1.0–1.6.
299:   `da_all_lines_decided_mono_axis` keeps only the items whose reference |Δ|
300:   clears 2 sds of the two-run difference; on the benchmark population that
301:   restriction does not help — the benchmarks' failure is not noise at the
302:   reference — while on `bpb_macro` and the loss it leaves one item per L,
303:   a 0/1 reading.
304: - Depth is a vanishing advantage, not a crossover (`depth_crossover.csv`,
305:   the batch-84 / batch-168 retrains): deep beats shallow by 1.9–2.1
306:   difference sds at 90M and 1.1–1.6 at 175M, by |z| ≤ 0.51 at 350M, shallow
307:   is ahead by ≤ 1.46 sds at 600M, and deep by 0.48–1.03 at 1B and 0.81–0.95
308:   at 1.7B — all inside noise from 350M on. The depth DA on the trained
309:   languages' BPB — 0.88–1.00 at 90M, 0.82–1.00 at 175M, 0.02–0.85 at 350M,
310:   0.00–0.08 at 600M, 0.47–0.92 at 1B over L8–L50 — reads a reference that
311:   has no real preference.
312: - On every language's BPB (`bpb_all`, the languages only one level trains
313:   included) the decided items are read well, but that population is
314:   dominated by "the model that saw the language wins" —
315:   [rq06](../rq06_language_transfer/README.md).
316: 
317: **Follow-ups**
318: 
319: - Items are not independent: a language's BPB items move together, so 37
320:   languages agreeing is closer to one decision measured 37 times than to 37
321:   decisions. Report the number of decisions (intervention × L) that agree
322:   with the item share as the secondary number, and bootstrap over L.
323: - The reference changes between lines (600M or 1B for some interventions,
324:   1.7B for others; `refs` in the table): name it in the legend, and re-read
325:   every line against 1.7B once the missing 1.7B cells finish.
326: - The seed sd is the median over the replicated deep scheme-A cells applied
327:   to every size and scheme, each on 3 seeds (the median-of-sd is biased low
328:   by ~17 %); use the size's own sd where the ×3 cells exist at the
329:   reference's size and widen the decided cut to cover its sampling error.
330: - The training loss is one item per L, so its line is a 0/0.5/1 step
331:   function; drop it from the paper version. `da_all_lines_flops_mono_axis` (5 × 3 lines of
332:   60 cells) is not readable; one line per size, or per-size markers.
