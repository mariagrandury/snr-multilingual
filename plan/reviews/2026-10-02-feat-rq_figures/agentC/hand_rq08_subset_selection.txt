1: # RQ8 — Can a subset of subtasks give higher SNR than the full set?
2: 
3: ## Research question
4: 
5: > Per benchmark, does a subset of subtasks — a subset of *languages* in a
6: > multilingual family, or a subset of *subjects* in MMLU, or a subset of
7: > individual *items* — give higher SNR than the full set, and does the same
8: > subset hold across seeds and scales?
9: 
19: 
20: ## Experimental setup
21: 
22: Subtask-level outputs under `pretraining/<pool>/` (ladder pool `predictivity`;
23: the 36-sweep pools as history); per-item (Option D) under
24: `per_sample/variance_prefilter/` (36-sweep only — it needs the per-sample
25: files that live on the cluster). Three subtask cases plus a per-item view;
26: on the ladder the multilingual families span up to 100 languages, so Case 1
27: asks which language subset of Belebele, Global-PIQA, MultiBLiMP or
28: Global-MMLU carries the family's signal, and the per-language BPB family
29: (`bpb`) is swept the same way (which languages' BPB make the sharpest
30: macro-average):
31: 
32: - **Case 1** — language subset of a multilingual family. `task` = the benchmark
33:   family (arc, belebele, global_mmlu, xnli, …); `subtask` = the per-language
34:   tasks in that family (arc_de, arc_es, …). Which language subset, ordered by
35:   per-language SNR, gives the highest combined SNR for the family?
36: - **Case 2** — MMLU subject subset (mean over the global_mmlu languages the
37:   pool carries, 37 on the ladder). `task` = `global_mmlu_full`; `subtask` = one
38:   subject (leaves only), whose per-(model, ckpt) score is the mean across languages.
39: - **Case 3** — MMLU subject subset per language (no cross-language averaging).
40:   `task` = `global_mmlu_full_<lang>`; `subtask` = one subject within that
41:   language.
42: - **Per-item (Option D)** — `per_sample/variance_prefilter/`. Subset selection at
43:   the individual-item level (per-sample SNR on binary item accuracy — not
44:   comparable to subtask-level SNR).
45: 
46: ## Methodology
47: 
48: - **Subset SNR.** `signal_to_noise_ratio` over per-*model* last-N-checkpoint
49:   arrays, one array per training run (seed replicates and external models
50:   are separate runs), with the noise pooled across runs as in upstream's
51:   `compute_snr_small_scale` (see the comparability note below). A
52:   combined subset averages the per-(model, step) scores across its subtasks
53:   first.
54: - **Sweep.** Per (task, size) the subtasks are ranked by standalone SNR and
55:   the cumulative prefixes 1..N are scored; `best_n` / `best_subset` is the
56:   prefix with the highest SNR and `snr_gain = best − full`.
57: - **Gate.** The rq00 above-random mask: a language task at chance at a size
58:   is not swept (Case 1), and a language whose `global_mmlu_full_<lang>`
59:   aggregate is at chance is skipped (Cases 2 and 3). The four MMLU category
60:   roll-ups are excluded from the subject lists.
61: - **Selection null.** The prefix is chosen on the same numbers it is scored
62:   on, so `best ≥ full` by construction. For every swept cell, 100 random
63:   subsets of the best size give `null_snr_p95`; `gain_over_null =
64:   best − null_p95` is the part of the gain that is not selection.

174: ## Results from the 36-model sweep (2026-06, superseded)
175: 
176: The numbers below were generated on the 36-model sweep (4 sizes × 3 data mixtures × 3 seeds, 12 languages, pool `custom_swissai_hf` unless stated) and are kept as history; the predictivity ladder regenerates the blocks above.
177: 
178: ### Highlighted result
179: 
180: - **`global_mmlu_full_vi` 1B (global_mmlu_full_per_language)** — a subset beats the full set: SNR **2.05 → 4.01** (**+1.95**) with `high_school_world_history|business_ethics|marketing`.
181: - **`global_mmlu_full` 175M (global_mmlu_full_subjects)** — a subset beats the full set: SNR **2.12 → 3.65** (**+1.52**) with `medical_genetics`.
182: - **`paws` 3B (per_benchmark)** — a subset beats the full set: SNR **0.37 → 1.81** (**+1.44**) with `paws_eu`.
183: - **MMLU subject subsets are the most/most-stable lever** — a 1–2 subject subset matches or beats the full ~48-subject set across sizes (`medical_genetics`, `human_aging`, `international_law`, world-history recur).
184: - **Per-item (per-sample) ranking is mostly noise / overfits across scale** — per-sample subsets give even larger gains but their best picks barely overlap across sizes (Jaccard ≈ 0.03, SNR-rank Spearman ≈ 0.05), so prefer subtask-level selection.
