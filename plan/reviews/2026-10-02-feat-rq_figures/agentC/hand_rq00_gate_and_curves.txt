32: ## Experimental setup
33: 
34: Curves under `pretraining/<pool>/{per_benchmark,per_language}/`: accuracy vs
35: FLOPs (log-x, `6 × (N_non_emb + d·V) × D`), one curve per language setting
36: (`plotted_mixes` in the `snr` config: L1 … L50, deep, scheme A, seed 1904)
37: and per size 90M–1.7B (each rung at its own batch, rule 10). Tasks are
38: parent-aggregated (subjects collapse into the parent; languages stay distinct); each task's **Signal** = (max−min)/mean of
39: the per-setting final scores at the reference size (1.7B, or the largest size with
40: data); only the top-3 families by Signal get curve grids. The 36-sweep pools
41: still draw their three data mixtures and overlay the external models to 70B.
42: 
43: The **above-random gate** ([`above_random.py`](above_random.py)) is
44: foundational and depends **only** on raw eval scores and the answer-option
45: counts — `n_options` in `configs/tasks.json` where it was derived from the
46: evaluated samples, the per-family table in that file otherwise — it reads no
47: RQ output, so every RQ depends on the gate, never the reverse. A run is above
48: chance on a task when the one-sided 95 % Wilson lower bound of its accuracy
49: over the task's `n_items` clears `1/n_options`, and a `(benchmark, size)` cell
50: is above random when at least half of the size's runs that train the language
51: are (`MIN_SHARE`). There is no fixed margin: the earlier
52: `mean score > 1/n_options + 0.05` rule is gone, so a 2-option task with 500
53: items and a 4-option task with 100 are held to the same evidence.
54: `run_apertus_snr_variants.py` NaN-s every at-chance cell so the gate propagates
55: to all downstream RQs. Per-language BPB and generative tasks have no chance
56: level and are never gated.

417: ## Custom vs. external: the at-chance problem is a capability artifact
418: 
419: *36-sweep numbers (pools `custom` and `external`, 2026-06, custom sizes
420: 175M–1B, RQ numbers of that layout); the ladder's counterpart is figure 4
421: above (`above_random_external`).*
422: 
423: This is the foundational result the rest of the paper rests on. The
424: above-random gate is identical for every model set, but what it removes depends
425: entirely on *who is being evaluated*.
426: 
427: **On the custom pretrains the gate is brutally selective.** Of 118 benchmarks,
428: only **44 clear chance at ≥1 size and 74 are random everywhere** — the 175M–1B
429: models we train are simply too weak to register signal on most translated
430: knowledge MCQA. The loss is concentrated in the 4-option families: only **9 / 63**
431: clear chance, so `belebele`, `global_mmlu_full`, `arc`, and `truthfulqa` are
432: NaN-ed out before any SNR is computed. The surviving pool is therefore *almost
433: entirely 2-option*, which is exactly what later biases the design-feature
434: analyses (RQ2/RQ5) toward "fewer options ⇒ higher SNR".
435: 
436: **Re-running the identical gate on the external tier dissolves the penalty.** The
437: `external` model set (every non-custom model — `reference_hf` + a06 + distillation
438: + posttraining, sizes 270M…70B; gate report in `all/external/`, curves skipped)
439: clears chance on **122 / 124** benchmarks, including **68 / 69** four-option
440: families. A 4-option translated benchmark is not intrinsically low-signal — it
441: only looks that way under models too small to beat chance.
442: 
443: | model set | models | benchmarks | above ≥1 size | 2-opt | 3-opt | 4-opt | 5-opt |
444: |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
445: | `custom` (SNR-gate domain) | 175M–1B custom pretrains | 118 | **44 (37%)** | 28 / 42 | 7 / 11 | **9 / 63** | 0 / 2 |
446: | `external` (`all/external`) | 270M–70B reference / a06 / distill / post | 124 | **122 (98%)** | 42 / 42 | 10 / 11 | **68 / 69** | 2 / 2 |
447: 
448: The same benchmark tells the story directly: on the `custom_swissai_hf`
449: acc-vs-FLOPs curves, Belebele sits flat at chance across the custom 175M–1B
450: sweep, then the overlaid external final-checkpoint markers climb steeply toward
451: 0.8+ out to 70B.
452: 
453: ![Belebele: custom at chance, externals climb to 0.8+](pretraining/custom_swissai_hf/per_benchmark/belebele.png)
454: 
455: **Implication for the paper.** The custom-pool gate measures *our models' scale*,
456: not benchmark reliability; the external tier is the control that separates the
457: two. This single distinction drives the external-tier findings downstream: option
458: count stops predicting SNR (RQ2), and 4-option HellaSwag becomes the
459: *highest*-SNR family once it clears the gate (RQ5).

463: ## Methodology — scaling beyond 1B (36-sweep pools)
464: 
465: The `custom_swissai_hf` pool extends the compute axis past the custom 1B ceiling
466: by folding in the apertus3 a06, distilled, and reference-HF trajectories. Three
467: mechanisms make that work:
468: 
469: - **Bucketed size axis.** The four custom sizes (175M / 350M / 600M / 1B) stay
470:   singletons; nearby external/large sizes pool into buckets so each holds ≥2
471:   models. A bucket with ≥2 models contributes a computable cross-model signal and
472:   decision accuracy; a singleton bucket (e.g. an isolated 70B) still plots its
473:   final-checkpoint marker on the curves but is dropped from the noise/DA pool.
474: - **Relative-fraction ckpt-DA.** External trajectories never hit the custom
475:   absolute megatron iters, so checkpoint-DA selects each model's early checkpoint
476:   at a *fraction* of its own max step rather than a fixed iter — letting the a06,
477:   distillation, SmolLM3, Olmo-3 and Apertus-8B series participate and extending
478:   ckpt-DA into the large buckets. (The computation lives in
479:   [compute_da.py](../rq02_decision_accuracy/compute_da.py); the gate and curves
480:   here just consume its trajectories.)
481: - **Cross-bucket scaling-DA is family-coverage-limited.** Scaling-DA auto-detects
482:   bucket pairs where ≥2 model *families* span both sizes (via within-family
483:   ladders such as gemma-3 / OLMo). Above 1B this is sparse — few families ship
484:   multiple sizes — so the few detectable pairs saturate at DA 1.0 on a handful of
485:   shared tasks: directionally useful, not yet statistically strong. acc-vs-FLOPs
486:   curves and the signal pool carry the >1B scaling story for now.
