1: # RQ6 — Does it generalize to languages we have not measured? (paper RQ5)
2: 
3: ## Research question
4: 
5: > If a language's BPB was measured on one or two small rungs only, can the
6: > exponent pooled over the other languages predict its BPB at the reference
7: > size — and does that hold for languages the model never trained on? The
8: > paper's RQ5
9: > ([`documents/paper/sections/04_analysis.tex`](../../../../documents/paper/sections/04_analysis.tex)).
10: > The folder also holds the detailed per-language BPB curves of every cell.
11: 
19: 
20: ## Experimental setup
21: 
22: Per-language BPB of the plan grid (deep, scheme A, seed 1904) at the final
23: checkpoint, from 90M up, at every L with at least four sizes (three proxy
24: rungs and a reference, the largest size at that L). Every one of the 100
25: validation languages is a series; "trained" means the language is in the
26: cell's FineWeb-2 list at that L (`launch_trainings.cell_fineweb_subsets`).
27: 
28: ## Methodology
29: 
30: - **Own exponent.** log BPB = a − α log N per language over the proxy rungs.
31: - **Leave-one-language-out.** For each language the exponent is the median
32:   of the other languages' α at that L; the intercept comes from the held-out
33:   language's k smallest rungs (k = 1 … all); the prediction at the reference
34:   is compared with the language's own fit (k ≥ 3) and with the largest proxy's
35:   BPB taken as is. Relative errors, medians per (trained, k).
36: - **Decision transfer.** rq05's final-checkpoint agreement on the
37:   `bpb_untrained` population against `bpb_trained` — whether a decision read
38:   on the trained languages also holds on the ones neither level trains.
39: - **BPB curves.** One panel per cell of the pool, per-language BPB against
40:   fraction of run: trained languages blue, unseen grey, macro BPB dashed
41:   (the progress report's `plot_bpb` on the analysis' cells).

98: **Key findings** (`transfer_da_all_lines_mono_axis`, `transfer_da_all_by_L_mono_axis`: rq05's per-item
99: agreement on the per-language BPB of all 100 evaluation languages, grouped
100: per (intervention, L) by what the two levels' lists do with the language —
101: both train it, only one does, neither does but one trains its script, or
102: neither trains even the script; the last two groups are the transfer test)
103: 
104: - The "trained languages read the decision at 1.0" reading is the languages
105:   only one list trains, where the model that saw the language wins at every
106:   size; it is the inclusion decision, not transfer.
107: - Script is a coarse proxy for relatedness (Latin covers Welsh and Vietnamese
108:   alike), and at L1 only English is trained, so "script trained" is every
109:   Latin-script language.
110: - `transfer_da_all_lines_mono_axis.csv` is still empty on the 2026-09-30 tables (the L8 and
111:   L30 list decisions against 1.7B do not reach the three items a panel needs
112:   at this snapshot either), so the per-size numbers of the earlier snapshot
113:   (script-trained languages read at 0.61–0.77 from 175M to 1B, unseen scripts
114:   0.33–0.73, neither clearing 0.75 below 1B) are not re-read here; they are
115:   quoted again once the figure draws.

169: **Key findings**
170: 
171: - English alone is the worst single-language proxy of the multilingual
172:   decision: pooled over the panel's benchmarks at 1B English reads 0.61
173:   against 0.62–0.67 for the other panel languages (de 0.67, it 0.65, zh 0.65,
174:   ru 0.64, es 0.63, fr 0.62; ja 0.55) and 0.67 for the panel macro; at 600M
175:   English (0.60) sits with Japanese and Chinese (0.56) at the bottom, below
176:   the macro's 0.66.
177: - Per benchmark the proxy's macro reaches 0.93 on hellaswag, 0.89 on LAMBADA
178:   and INCLUDE-rfgm and 0.81 on Global-MMLU-rf at 1B, where English alone reads
179:   0.87, 0.78, — and 0.69; on `multiblimp` English reads 0.40 against 0.68 for
180:   the macro. Where the macro lies above every single language (hellaswag,
181:   Global-MMLU-rf, INCLUDE-rf/rfgm, xstorycloze) the languages' errors are
182:   partly independent and the panel is worth evaluating; on `xnli` and
183:   `xwinograd` nothing reads the reference at 1B.
184: - A developer who evaluates a multilingual recipe on English benchmarks
185:   alone misreads the multilingual decision more often than one who evaluates
186:   any other single panel language; the macro over the readable panel is the
187:   safest proxy at every size.
