1: # Task reformulation for the auto evals
2: 
3: Status 2026-09-18. Companion to [plan/benchmark_selection.md](../../../../plan/benchmark_selection.md)
4: (what we evaluate on) and [rq00_gate_and_curves](../rq00_gate_and_curves/) (the chance gate). The
5: reference implementation is [lighteval_reference.py](lighteval_reference.py)
6: (HuggingFace's FineWeb-edu ablation tasks) — it is a *reference*: everything
7: here runs inside the lm-eval harness we already use.
8: 
9: ## Why
10: 
11: Three quarters of the auto suite is at chance at these sizes: 113 of 461
12: gated tasks clear chance at any size, and only 21 of 252 four-option tasks;
13: `global_mmlu_full`, `global_piqa_*` and `truthfulqa-multi_mc1` never do.
14: Base models at 90M–1.7B do not pick up the "answer with a letter" convention,
15: so a lettered multiple-choice prompt measures the convention, not the
16: knowledge. The lighteval file's fix for MMLU: no letters, no few-shot, the
17: full answer string scored as the continuation after `Answer:`, `acc_norm`
18: (length-normalised) as the metric.
19:

51: ## Tier 1 — programmatic reformulation (implemented)
52: 
53: [`src/evals/scripts/make_rf_tasks.py`](../../../evals/scripts/make_rf_tasks.py) writes one self-contained harness YAML
54: per original task under `src/evals/tasks/rf/<family>/rf_<task>.yaml` — same
55: `dataset_path`, `dataset_name` and split, read from the pinned harness
56: checkout — with:
57: 
58: | family | `doc_to_text` | `doc_to_choice` |
59: |---|---|---|
60: | belebele | `{passage}\nQuestion: {question}\nAnswer:` | the four `mc_answer*` strings |
61: | global_mmlu_full | `The following are questions about {subject}.\nQuestion: {question}\nAnswer:` | `option_a..d` |
62: | include_base_44 | `Question: {question}\nAnswer:` | `option_a..d` |
63: 
64: `output_type: multiple_choice`, `num_fewshot: 0`, metrics `acc` and
65: `acc_norm`. The global_mmlu and include twins are one task per language over
66: the whole split rather than a group of subject/domain subtasks — the report
67: aggregates to the family anyway, and a 57-way group is what made the
68: originals slow.
69: 
70: Plumbing, all keyed on the new task names so originals and twins coexist:
71: 
72: - `configs/tasks.json`: one entry per twin (`language`, `benchmark:
73:   rf_<family>`, `n_options: 4`, `metric: acc_norm`), group `auto_rf`,
74:   `benchmarks` metadata. The `rf_` **prefix** is deliberate:
75:   `tasks_for_benchmarks` matches `<benchmark>_…`, so a `_rf` suffix would be
76:   selected by the original family. Registered entries are what keep the
77:   analysis from folding `rf_belebele_eng_Latn` into `belebele` (`results_io.
78:   aggregate_parents`, `analysis/utils._is_language_aggregate`) and what gives
79:   `ladder_report` its `chance` column.
80: - `evaluate.sbatch`: `HARNESS_INCLUDE_PATH` → `eval_worker.py --include_path`
81:   (already accepted, never set before). The pinned wheel is untouched.
82: - `auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated`: the `auto_rf` group instead of `auto`,
83:   jobs named `eval-<cell>-iter<N>-rf` (the original and the rf set of one
84:   checkpoint are different work — neither watcher may read the other's job
85:   as its own); the include path is always exported. Per-task idempotency, walltime sizing,
86:   hold-backs and the W&B push need nothing — the metric override makes the
87:   series `rf_<task>/acc_norm`.
88: - Per cell: L1 2 · L2 5 · L8 24 · L15 49 · L30 84 · L50 124 rf tasks (185 in
89:   every language).

91: ### First evals to launch
92: 
93: The question is whether the reformulation moves scores off the chance line
94: at all, and whether it opens a gap between sizes. Answer it on final
95: checkpoints before touching the grid:
96: 
97: 1. **Two checkpoints, one job each**: the strongest model and a small one at
98:    the same setting — `lm-1.7B-L8-deep-seed1904` and `lm-175M-L8-deep-seed1904`,
99:    final iter plus the noise window (`--every 1000` coarsens the tenths away, but the 85/90/95/100 % points are added unconditionally, so five saves are due, not one). Both are
100:    ALL_LANGUAGES runs, so every rf task runs (185) and the comparison covers
101:    trained and untrained languages. Read: per family and language, rf
102:    `acc_norm` vs the original `acc` on the same checkpoint (both on disk),
103:    against chance 0.25 + 0.05. If the 1.7B stays at chance, stop: the
104:    families need Tier 2, not a prompt change.
105: 2. **The L8 size ladder finals** (90M … 1.7B, 6 jobs): does rf order the
106:    sizes, and from which size does it clear the gate?
107: 3. **The deep-A-seed1904 ladder** at the normal `--every 2` cadence, under
108:    `--watch`, throttled with `--max-submit`.
109: 
110: ```bash
111: cd /iopsstor/scratch/cscs/mariagrandury/Projects/snr-multilingual/src/pretrain
112: # 1. dry-run, then two jobs
113: python3.11 auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated --name lm-1.7B-L8-deep-seed1904 --every 1000 --dry-run
114: python3.11 auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated --name lm-1.7B-L8-deep-seed1904 --every 1000
115: python3.11 auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated --name lm-175M-L8-deep-seed1904 --every 1000
116: # 2. the L8 finals of every size
117: for s in 90M 175M 350M 600M 1B 1.7B; do
118:   python3.11 auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated --name lm-$s-L8-deep-seed1904 --every 1000
119: done
120: # 3. the whole seed-1904 deep ladder, every 2nd checkpoint
121: python3.11 auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated --arch deep --scheme A --seed 1904 --max-submit 20 --watch 1800
122: ```
123: 
124: The walltime estimate is fitted on the original task mix; the rf
125: global_mmlu tasks score four answer strings per question over 14k questions,
126: so the first jobs may hit their limit. That costs only the tasks in flight:
127: the next pass resubmits what is missing, sized to it.

129: ### Which metric to compare
130: 
131: The originals ask for a letter, so every option is one token of the same
132: length (" A" … " D") and byte-length normalisation cannot change the ranking:
133: their `acc` and `acc_norm` are identical, and comparing the rf `acc_norm`
134: with the original `acc` is acc_norm against acc_norm. The rf tasks are the
135: only place the two differ — the answers have different lengths — and
136: `acc_norm` is the standard choice for cloze scoring (the lighteval reference
137: scores `loglikelihood_acc_norm`). It is the metric the pipeline carries for
138: the rf tasks: tasks.json's per-task `metric`, honoured by `results_io.flatten`
139: (W&B) and `ladder_report._primary` (the report), so both show the same number.
140: `compare.py` reads that `primary_score` from the ladder report through the
141: rq00 gate (`above_random.scores_and_mask` on the `predictivity` pool, deep
142: scheme-A cells): so the table below is `original acc → rf acc_norm`, and it
143: moves only after `ladder_report.py --plot --publish --push-hf` has picked up
144: new rf results and `scripts/refresh_analysis.sh` has re-run. Until then `compare.py` refuses to run against a report without the `rf_*` columns (it would empty the table); point `SNR_LADDER_DIR` at a report that has them. The conclusion
145: does not depend on the metric — in the pilot at 1.7B in the trained
146: languages belebele gains +0.14 on acc_norm and +0.16 on acc, Global-MMLU
147: +0.08 and +0.06 (the per-task `acc` stays in the eval logs and W&B).

171: ## Tier 2 — rewriting the items with Gemini (`rfgm_*`, driver built, data not yet produced)
172: 
173: Tier 1 only drops the letters. Here the item itself changes: Gemini turns
174: the question into one declarative sentence that stops where the answer goes
175: (a stem) and the four options into short, parallel continuations, in the
176: item's own language — the cloze formulation the FineWeb-2 / FineTasks work
177: found readable at small scale. Decided 2026-09-19: the three letter families
178: in all their languages (185 tasks, ~636k items), `gemini-3.8-flash` through
179: the Batch API, evaluated on the deep scheme-A seed-1904 ladder at every
180: evaluated checkpoint (the `rf_*` coverage), so original / rf / rfgm are
181: compared on identical models. The families that are cloze already (arc,
182: hellaswag, global_piqa) are not rewritten.
183: 
184: The set is a third family prefix — `rfgm_` — on the exact `rf_` plumbing:
185: [`rewrite_items_gemini.py`](../../../evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py)
186: produces one JSONL per task under
187: `/capstor/store/cscs/swissai/infra01/msnr/msnr-harness/rf-data/rfgm/` (never
188: pushed publicly: gold labels), `make_rf_tasks.py --set rfgm` writes one
189: `dataset_path: json` YAML per task under `src/evals/tasks/rfgm/` and the
190: tasks.json entries (`benchmark: rfgm_<family>`, `metric: acc_norm`, group
191: `auto_rfgm`), `auto_evals_cscs.py --reformulated rfgm` evaluates them with
192: `-rfgm` job names, and `compare.py` draws them next to `rf` (SETS).
193: 
194: Row schema of `<task>.jsonl` (`text`, `choices`, `gold` are what the YAML
195: reads by column name; the rest is for audits):
196: 
197: ```json
198: {"id": 17, "text": "<passage>\n<stem>", "choices": ["…", "…", "…", "…"], "gold": 2,
199:  "stem": "…", "context": "<passage or ''>", "subject": "anatomy|''",
200:  "orig_question": "…", "orig_choices": ["…"], "lang": "deu_Latn"}
201: ```
202: 
203: `text` is the full prompt: the belebele passage verbatim + newline + stem;
204: Global-MMLU / INCLUDE the stem only. Gold keeps the original position. The
205: stem carries no trailing space — the harness appends `" " + choice` itself.

207: ### Step by step
208: 
209: **0. Once — Application Default Credentials.** We authenticate to Vertex AI
210: with ADC, not an API key: the Generative Language API refuses keys under an
211: organisation policy that blocks them, which is what `API_KEY_INVALID` on a
212: well-formed key means. ADC uses your own Google identity and the SDK picks
213: it up with no secret in the repo. Run Google's
214: [setup script](https://storage.googleapis.com/cloud-samples-data/adc/setup_adc.sh)
215: — it installs the gcloud CLI, asks for your project ID, runs the login,
216: sets the quota project and enables `aiplatform.googleapis.com` — or do the
217: same by hand:
218: 
219: ```bash
220: curl -sSL https://sdk.cloud.google.com | bash && exec -l $SHELL   # gcloud, into $HOME
221: gcloud auth application-default login --no-browser                # prints a command to run on your laptop
222: gcloud auth application-default set-quota-project <project-id>
223: ```
224: 
225: **Run the login on the login node, not on your laptop.** ADC is a file,
226: `~/.config/gcloud/application_default_credentials.json`, and the driver
227: reads the one on the machine it runs on. `--no-browser` prints a
228: `gcloud … --remote-bootstrap="…"` command to paste into a laptop that has
229: gcloud and a browser; that returns a URL you paste back. If you already
230: authenticated on the laptop, copying the file over is equivalent and
231: quicker: `scp ~/.config/gcloud/application_default_credentials.json
232: clariden:.config/gcloud/`. Credentials refresh themselves and gcloud is not
233: needed again afterwards.
234: 
235: **Enabling the API is a different credential.** `gcloud auth
236: application-default login` writes ADC for client libraries; it does not log
237: the gcloud CLI itself in, so `gcloud services enable` answers "You do not
238: currently have an active account selected". Either run `gcloud auth login`
239: first, or just switch the API on in the console:
240: `console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/aiplatform.googleapis.com?project=<project-id>`.
241: The project also needs billing — batch prediction is not a free-tier
242: feature.
243: 
244: **Cloud Storage.** A Vertex batch job reads its requests from Cloud Storage
245: and writes its answers back there (an uploaded file, which the Gemini
246: Developer API takes, is not a valid Vertex source). The driver keeps them
247: under `gs://<bucket>/rfgm/`, where `<bucket>` is `$RFGM_GCS_BUCKET`, else
248: `--bucket`, else `<project>-msnr-rfgm`, and creates it on first use with
249: the same ADC credential — which needs `storage.buckets.create` on the
250: project (`roles/storage.admin`, or `roles/storage.bucketCreator` plus
251: `roles/storage.objectAdmin`). Without it the driver stops with the 403 and
252: the role to ask for.
253: 
254: ```bash
255: # 1. can this account create the bucket?
256: gcloud storage buckets create gs://$GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT-msnr-rfgm \
257:     --project=$GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT --location=US --uniform-bucket-level-access
258: # 2. if that is a 403, a project admin runs ONE of:
259: gcloud projects add-iam-policy-binding $GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT \
260:     --member=user:<you@example.org> --role=roles/storage.admin     # then repeat step 1
261: gcloud storage buckets create gs://<name> --project=$GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT --location=US
262: gcloud storage buckets add-iam-policy-binding gs://<name> \
263:     --member=user:<you@example.org> --role=roles/storage.objectAdmin   # then export RFGM_GCS_BUCKET=<name>
264: # 3. verify
265: gcloud storage ls gs://$GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT-msnr-rfgm
266: ```
267: 
268: The bucket may live in another project the account does own; Vertex reads
269: it as long as the caller has object access. Only `submit`/`fetch` need it —
270: `pilot` and `build` do not. About 250 MB of requests and a similar volume of
271: answers, at Standard-class prices a few cents a month; delete the bucket
272: when the rewritten sets are on capstor.
273: 
274: **The environment every command below starts from.** The location must be
275: `global`: the whole Gemini 3.x family is served only from the global
276: endpoint, and a regional job answers 404 *The PublisherModel does not
277: exist* (checked 2026-09-20 against `us-central1`, `europe-west4` and
278: `us-east5` — only 2.5 is regional). Batch prediction accepts `global`, and
279: the bucket's `US` multi-region default serves it.
280: `google-genai` is in `requirements-ml.txt` and already in the snr env; the
281: storage calls use `google-auth` and `requests`, which come with it.
282: 
283: ```bash
284: cd /iopsstor/scratch/cscs/mariagrandury/Projects/snr-multilingual
285: export GOOGLE_GENAI_USE_VERTEXAI=true
286: export GOOGLE_CLOUD_PROJECT=<project-id> GOOGLE_CLOUD_LOCATION=global
287: export HF_HOME=/iopsstor/scratch/cscs/mariagrandury/hf_home HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 HF_DATASETS_OFFLINE=1
288: ```
289: 
290: `GOOGLE_GENAI_USE_VERTEXAI` decides the shape of the request files, so the
291: driver refuses to run if the SDK resolves a different backend than the one
292: it was configured for — export it before `build`, not between `build` and
293: `submit`. Everything runs on the login node (outbound internet, the snr
294: env, the offline datasets cache the items come from); the driver is
295: network-bound and light, and every mode is idempotent, so re-run after any
296: interruption. Vertex's batch quota is per project and region: `submit`
297: stops at the first `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` and is re-run later.
298: 
299: **1. Pilot (~$0.40, 15 min).** Synchronous calls with the batch's prompt
300: and schema on 10 items of each review task — the `REVIEW_TASKS` in the
301: driver: Spanish, Hindi, Turkish, Farsi, Greek, Arabic, Basque and Chinese
302: in every family that has them (8 belebele, 7 Global-MMLU, 8 INCLUDE; 230
303: items) — printed original → rewritten and written to `pilot_<task>.jsonl`
304: in this directory (accepted rows in the row schema, rejected ones with
305: their reason):
306: 
307: ```bash
308: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py pilot
309: ```
310: 
311: Read all of them: language kept, stem answerable, gold not leaked, options
312: parallel. The prompt (`SYSTEM`, `NOTES` in the driver) is tuned here and
313: nowhere else; the validator should pass ≥ 95 %. `--tasks a,b` / `--family`
314: narrow the pilot while iterating on the prompt.
315: 
316: **2. Build the request files.** One line per item in
317: `rf-data/rfgm/_requests/<task>.jsonl` (REST JSON: the user content, the
318: system instruction, `responseSchema`, `thinkingLevel: LOW`), rows with a
319: missing option dropped as in the rf twins; prints the token estimate and
320: the cost per family. `--dry-run` prints the estimate only.
321: 
322: ```bash
323: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py build --dry-run
324: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py build
325: ```
326: 
327: **3a. No bucket? Run it online instead.** `online` uses the same prompt and
328: the same validation but calls `generate_content` directly, so it needs
329: nothing but ADC — no Cloud Storage, no `build`/`submit`/`fetch`. It costs
330: **double** the batch price and takes wall-clock time instead of a queue,
331: so it is the fallback while `storage.buckets.create` is missing, not the
332: default.
333: 
334: ```bash
335: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py online --family include_base_44 --L 50
336: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py online --family belebele --L 50
337: ```
338: 
339: **`--L 50` is not an optimisation, it is the right task set.** The ladder
340: only scores a task on models that trained its language (rule 2,
341: `utils.trained_only`), so a twin outside the L-cell's languages is paid for
342: and never read — the rq00 gate's own task counts are exactly this set. L50
343: keeps 124 of the 185 tasks:
344: 
345: | family | all | L50 | items (L50) |
346: |---|---:|---:|---:|
347: | belebele | 105 | 59 | 53,100 |
348: | global_mmlu_full | 37 | 29 | 407,192 |
349: | include_base_44 | 43 | 36 | 18,337 |
350: 
351: On belebele that is $38 and ~3 hours saved online; on Global-MMLU, 112k
352: items. `--scheme` picks the scheme `--L` refers to (default A).
353: 
354: Run one family at a time. Two concurrent runs at the default concurrency
355: put 24 requests in flight, which is where the shared quota collapses — the
356: aggregate is slower than running them in sequence.
357: 
358: Every answer is appended and flushed as it lands and a re-run skips the ids
359: already written, so a killed login-node process loses at most the requests
360: in flight — re-run the same command to continue. `--limit N` caps new items
361: per task, `--concurrency` defaults to 12: Gemini 3.x online runs on Vertex's
362: dynamic shared quota, where 12 threads sustain ~2.9 items/s while 24 trips
363: 429s and collapses throughput to 0.3 (measured 2026-09-20). It prints rate,
364: ETA and running cost every 200 items.
365: 
366: **3. Submit.** One batch job per task (185 files of 1–20 MB): each is
367: uploaded to `gs://<bucket>/rfgm/requests/<task>.jsonl` and becomes the
368: job's source, and the answers land in `…/<task>/dest`. Both URIs and the
369: job name go into `rf-data/rfgm/_jobs.json`; a task that has a job is
370: skipped, so re-run until `status` shows 185.
371: 
372: ```bash
373: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py submit --max-jobs 40
374: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py status
375: ```
376: 
377: **4. Fetch and validate.** Reads every SUCCEEDED job's destination prefix
378: in Cloud Storage, matches each answer back to its item — Vertex drops
379: everything outside `request` and echoes the request instead of a key, so
380: the match is on the prompt text, and any line that matches nothing is
381: counted and skipped rather than written — validates each item — JSON with a non-empty stem (a
382: trailing colon, dash or ellipsis is trimmed rather than rejected: five of
383: the six pilot rejects were a colon, and they fell on Basque 3/10 and
384: Persian 2/20 against 0 of 180 elsewhere, a per-language item loss this
385: comparison cannot absorb), exactly four non-empty pairwise-distinct
386: choices, no choice appearing as a word in the stem (answer leak), the
387: majority Unicode script unchanged, plus a per-choice script check for the
388: options the source wrote in the item's own script (the per-item majority was
389: blind to a Greek item whose four numerals came back in Latin; options that
390: were already off-script, such as a bare `"a, b"`, are skipped). Kana,
391: katakana, hangul and han count as one script: Japanese mixes kanji and kana
392: within a sentence, and comparing the per-choice majority without folding
393: them rejected 30.9 % of Japanese INCLUDE items against 0-2 % elsewhere — an
394: uneven item loss across languages is the one bias this comparison cannot
395: absorb. Script is the cheap "still in its language" check and cannot tell
396: Spanish from English; step 5 covers the Latin-script languages. Writes
397: `<task>.jsonl` (accepted) and `_rejects/<task>.jsonl` (with the reason and
398: what the model produced, so a reject can be re-judged without paying for the
399: call again; `online --retry-rejects` re-runs them). A task file is written only from a
400: SUCCEEDED job, so a partial run never leaves a half file; FAILED / EXPIRED
401: jobs are cleared and `submit` resubmits them (from the object already in
402: the bucket); rejected items get one more
403: round through `submit --retry` (then `fetch` again), a second rejection
404: drops the item. Batch jobs target 24 h; most finish sooner.
405: 
406: ```bash
407: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py fetch
408: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py submit --retry && python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py fetch
409: ```

411: **5. Spot-check (45 min, human).** Per task the row and reject counts,
412: reject rate, median stem / choice length, the tokens actually spent and
413: their cost, and with `--show` five random items of each of the 23 review
414: tasks (the pilot's eight languages):

416: ```bash
417: python3.11 src/evals/scripts/rewrite_items_gemini.py report --show
418: ```

421: collapsed to one word where the original was a clause. A task above 5 %
422: rejects: read its rejects before evaluating it.

497: that moves it. `scaffold()` estimates it as bytes/3.5, which runs ~30 %
498: high on English prose (719 vs 550), so `build --dry-run` moves with the
499: prompt but reads high. No cache discount is
500: available against it: implicit caching wants a 2 048-token shared prefix on
501: Vertex and this is a quarter of that, and batch and cache discounts would
502: not compound anyway.
503: 
504: `gemini-3.8-flash` batch: $0.375 / $1.875 per Mtok in / out through
505: 2026-12-31, double from 2027 — the same on Vertex as on the Gemini API
506: ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), billed to the
507: Cloud project instead of the key;
508: thinking tokens bill as output and no Gemini 3 model turns thinking off,
509: only down to `LOW` / `MINIMAL`. Input = item + ~150 tokens of instruction
510: and schema; output = stem + four choices + JSON; belebele passages are
511: input only. Bytes/3.5 estimates, ±30 %. `build --dry-run` prints the same
512: table from the cache; `report` prints what was actually spent. Cheaper
513: alternatives at the same scope: `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` batch
514: ($0.125 / $0.75 → ~$125–170), riskier on low-resource scripts;
515: `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` batch ($1 / $6 → ~$1,000–1,400).

517: ### The prompt
518: 
519: **The rewriter is never told which option is correct.** The user turn carries
520: the language, the subject, the passage, the question and the four options in
521: their original order, and nothing else; `gold` stays in the driver and is
522: copied to the output row untouched. Telling it would invite the failure that
523: defeats the whole exercise: a model that knows the answer writes the true
524: statement more fully or more specifically than the false ones, and a small
525: model then scores above chance by following the style rather than the
526: content, which is the letter-format artefact traded for a subtler one. The
527: instruction to "preserve which choice is correct" is a prohibition on
528: flipping truth values, not a disclosure.
529: 
530: It can still often infer the answer, so `report` measures what is left: how
531: often the gold continuation is the single longest of the four, in the
532: rewrite and in the originals side by side. Chance is 25 %, the originals
533: already carry some of this artefact, and what matters is whether the rewrite
534: raised it.
535: 
536: System instruction (`SYSTEM` in the driver; `{family_note}` per family):
537: 
538: ```
539: You rewrite multiple-choice test items so that a small language model can be
540: scored on them as text continuations. Rewrite the item in the SAME LANGUAGE
541: and script as the input — never translate, never switch to English.
542: 
543: Turn the question into ONE declarative sentence that stops exactly where the
544: answer would go (a "stem"). Turn each of the four options into a short
545: continuation that completes the stem into a true or false statement. Rules:
546: - The stem must not contain or hint at the correct answer, and must not say
547:   "which of the following".
548: - Keep the meaning of the original question and of every option; keep the
549:   options in the same order; keep the correct option correct.
550: - Make the four continuations parallel: same grammatical form, similar
551:   length (aim for 1-8 words), no option letters or numbers.
552: - The stem ends with no trailing space or punctuation; each continuation
553:   starts as it would follow a space after the stem (lowercase unless it is a
554:   name), and ends with a period if the stem+continuation is a full sentence.
555: - Do not add facts. If the question asks for the option that is NOT true,
556:   write the stem as "Of the following, the one that is not … is".
557: {family_note}
558: Return only JSON: {"stem": "...", "choices": ["...", "...", "...", "..."]}
559: ```
560: 
561: Family notes — belebele: "A passage is given; the stem must be answerable
562: from that passage alone. Do not rewrite or quote the passage." Global-MMLU:
563: "The subject is given; the stem may name it." INCLUDE: "The item may test
564: regional knowledge (driving rules, local history); keep the local terms."
565: User content per item: `Language:`, `Subject:` (Global-MMLU, INCLUDE),
566: `Passage:` (belebele), `Question:`, `Options:` numbered 1–4. The response
567: schema pins `{"stem": string, "choices": [4 strings]}`.

589: ### The length tell, and where it actually is
590: 
591: Replacing letters with answer strings hands the model a lever the lettered
592: form does not have: the options now differ in length. If the gold answer is
593: systematically the shortest (or longest) of its set, a model scores above
594: chance by preferring short (raw log-likelihood) or long (`acc_norm`, which
595: divides by length) strings without reading the stem — and the
596: original→`rf_` difference is then partly that artefact rather than the format
597: change. `acc_norm` normalises a candidate by its own length; it does not
598: remove a dataset-level correlation between *being the gold* and *being short*.
599: 
600: Measured off the cached items, not the model: how often the gold is the
601: **strictly** shortest (or longest) option, ties excluded, over every task of a
602: family (8 sampled where a family has more).
603: 
604: | family | chance | gold shortest | gold longest | items |
605: |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
606: | rf_acp_bench_mcq | 25.0 % | **35.4 %** | 15.1 % | 900 |
607: | rf_bbh_mcq | 23.5 % | 13.1 % | 10.9 % | 1,896 |
608: | rf_belebele | 25.0 % | 23.7 % | 20.8 % | 7,200 |
609: | rf_commonsense_qa | 20.0 % | 11.1 % | 19.0 % | 1,221 |
610: | rf_cultural_bench_easy | 25.0 % | 24.0 % | 27.1 % | 221 |
611: | rf_global_mmlu_full | 25.0 % | 17.8 % | 23.3 % | 112,332 |
612: | rf_include_base_44 | 25.0 % | 15.8 % | 22.2 % | 4,328 |
613: | rf_mmlu | 25.0 % | 17.8 % | 23.7 % | 14,042 |
614: | rfgm_include_base_44 | 25.0 % | 17.6 % | 21.9 % | 4,243 |
615: 
616: So this is not a property of the reformulation: every family the study
617: already relies on sits at or below chance on both tells, the Gemini rewrite
618: included (17.6 %, which is what the driver's own length report is there to
619: keep). It is a property of ACP-Bench's published distractor sets — its items
620: are plans, and a wrong plan tends to be stated at greater length — and it
621: only becomes reachable once the strings are scored. Three of its seven
622: subtasks carry it:
623: 
624: | task | chance | gold shortest |
625: |---|---:|---:|
626: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_areach | 25 % | 50.0 % |
627: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_val | 25 % | 47.7 % |
628: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_prog | 25 % | 46.9 % |
629: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_land | 25 % | 30.0 % |
630: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_app | 25 % | 28.5 % |
631: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_reach | 25 % | 26.9 % |
632: | rf_acp_bench_mcq_just | 25 % | 19.2 % |
633: 
634: For those three, the reference a score has to beat is the pick-shortest rate
635: (0.50 / 0.48 / 0.47), not the 0.25 the gate uses, and the gate's verdict on
636: them should be read with that substitution — which is why the probe reports
637: `rf_acp_bench_mcq` as a family and not as one number. `rf_bbh_mcq_snarks`,
638: the two-way task where a tell would be cheapest, measures 54.8 % against its
639: 50 % chance on 177 items, inside its own sampling error.
