1: # RQ2 — Does a benchmark rank models at a small size, or early in a run, the way the reference does?
2: 
3: ## Research question
4: 
5: > Decision accuracy (DA) is the ground truth of the whole framework: the
6: > probability that a benchmark orders a pair of models the way an evaluation
7: > of larger models would (Heineman et al., 2025). Which benchmarks, in which
8: > languages, keep the ranking of the ladder's design variants across sizes
9: > (**DA-size**), across training (**DA-ckpt**), and early *and* small at once
10: > (**DA-goal**)?
11: 
20: 
21: **In one paragraph** (ladder-report snapshot **2026-09-30 23:54**, the one
22: the tables on disk were built from — every cell of the grid evaluated except
23: FWEB and the 3B L30/L50 rungs, the 90M and 175M rungs at their own batch;
24: every number below is read from the CSV beside the figure it describes).
25: Ranking design variants from a smaller fully trained model is close to a
26: coin flip on the full gated population (0.54 at 90M, 0.55 at 175M, 0.56 at
27: 1B, jackknife ± 0.02); the 0.64 → 0.76 rise of the paper's filtered figure
28: (0.72 → 0.76 from 175M) is a conditional statement on the tasks whose
29: DA-size cleared 0.66. Ranking from an early checkpoint of the same run
30: reaches 0.74–0.82 at 90 % of training (0.75 at 90M, 0.82 at 350M), but two
31: seeds of one design reach up to 0.75 on the same axis (the seed null,
32: 0.56–0.58 on average): most of DA-ckpt's rise is within-run persistence, and
33: on English at 175M–1B two seeds of the same designs disagree with each other
34: (test-retest 0.55–0.89, 0.63–0.73 on average) as much as the proxy disagrees
35: with the reference. Restricting to the eight high-resource languages, to a
36: common task set, or to one language or one language tier does not order the
37: per-L lines, and a language's token share does not predict how reliably its
38: benchmarks rank (Spearman ρ 0.02 to 0.27 over 45–48 languages). Decision
39: accuracy, Kendall's τ and Spearman's ρ are one statistic (r ≥ 0.96 over
40: 1 883 cells), and the tie convention alone moves the reliable-task verdict
41: on 2–7 % of cells.

64: ## Setup
65: 
66: **Pools and sizes.** Models are the predictivity ladder's cells (pool
67: `predictivity`: 90M–1.7B, each rung at its own batch, rule 10;
68: L ∈ {1, 2, 8, 15, 30, 50} × deep/shallow × scheme A/B, seed 1904).
69: `predictivity_schemes` adds the AT3, ZH and ES cells at the grid seed and is
70: the pair set every decision figure from figure 1 on is computed over
71: (`reliable_tasks.py`, `by_L.py`, `scale_convergence.py` and the extensions;
72: rule 15); `predictivity_seeds` adds the replicate seeds as separate models
73: (figure 7). The gate pool is `predictivity` everywhere; the output folder is
74: `pretraining/predictivity/`. The reference is 1.7B (rule 9).
75: 
76: **Three decision accuracies.** DA is the share of design-variant pairs a
77: proxy orders the same way as the reference; a pair tied on both sides counts
78: as agreement and a pair tied on one side as a miss (the kernel's one departure
79: from upstream, below). **DA-size** compares the proxy size's final checkpoint
80: with the 1.7B final; **DA-ckpt** an early checkpoint of a run with that run's
81: own final; **DA-goal** an early checkpoint of the proxy with the 1.7B final.
82: Two identities are verified on every run: DA-size equals DA-goal at 100 % of
83: the proxy's run, and DA-ckpt of the 1.7B run equals DA-goal of the 1.7B run.
84: Pooled figures (`scale_convergence_da_size*`, `rq2_da_all*`, `reliability_da_size*`) report
85: matching decisions over comparable decisions summed over tasks; the
86: `early_small_da_*` families (`early_small.py`, `by_L.py`) report the mean over tasks. The two agree
87: to two decimals on today's tables but are different estimands.
88: 
89: **Pair sets (rule 15).** `multi-axis`: every pair of design variants at the
90: grid seed (two thirds move more than one axis at once). `mono-axis`
91: (`_mono_axis` stems; the multi-axis files carry `_multi_axes`): the pairs moving exactly one of L, depth, list, T,
92: lang2 — the decision a practitioner makes, and what upstream's "every pair"
93: is by construction. `seed`: two draws of one design (the null; DA-ckpt only,
94: figure 7). At the reference the mono-axis pairs are `language count` 39,
95: `depth` 10, `language list` 6, `temperature` 4 and `second language` 3; the
96: other 214 of the 276 pairs move two or more axes.
97: 
98: **Filters.** An `above_*` stem keeps the tasks whose DA cleared a cut on a
99: reduction of their cells (`reliable_tasks.py`): `above_66_size` /
100: `above_66_ckpt` / `above_66_either` (median DA over the proxy cells ≥ 0.66
101: on that axis, or on either), `above_66_both` (both axes; never quoted, RULES.md),
102: `above_80` (0.80 on the `late` reduction, both axes). Every filtered figure
103: plots the quantity it selected on and is read as conditional (bug #17,
104: `../CLAUDE.md`); the plain stem is the unconditional one.

106: ## Experimental setup
107: 
108: Models are the predictivity ladder's cells (`configs/models.json` pool
109: `predictivity`: 90M–1.7B (each rung at its own batch, rule 10) ×
110: L ∈ {1, 2, 8, 15, 30, 50} × deep/shallow × scheme A/B, seed 1904;
111: `predictivity_seeds` adds the seed replicates as separate models;
112: `predictivity_schemes` adds the AT3, ZH and ES cells at the grid seed and is
113: the pool `reliable_tasks.py`, `by_L.py` and `scale_convergence.py` pair over,
114: rule 15). The cross-size identity is the cell's `family`
115: (`lm-L8-schemeB-deep-seed1904`: everything but the size), so a design variant
116: present at two sizes is one pair.
117: 
118: - **DA-size** — `decision_acc_size_<small>`: the families' ranking at
119:   `<small>`'s final checkpoint vs at the reference size's (1.7B) final checkpoint;
120:   `decision_acc_size_<a>_to_<b>` for every other size pair with ≥ 2 shared
121:   families (175M→350M … 600M→1B). Multilingual tasks are only evaluated on
122:   cells that train the language, so each task's pair set is the families
123:   that exist at both sizes *and* were evaluated on it.
124: - **DA-ckpt** — `decision_acc_ckpt_f<frac>_<size>`: within one size, the
125:   ranking at the checkpoint nearest 10, 20, … 90 % of each run
126:   (`da_early_fracs`; 0.5C … 4.5C on the WSD schedule) vs at the final
127:   checkpoint.
128: 
129: Per-language BPB (`bpb_<subset>`) and the training loss are tasks too, so DA
130: is computed for the plan's outcome metric alongside the benchmarks. DA is
131: never gated: it is the truth the SNR proxies of rq03 and rq04 are scored against.

133: ## Methodology
134: 
135: [`compute_da.py`](compute_da.py) writes `pretraining/<pool>/da_all_per_task_both_axes.csv`
136: (one row per (parent task, pair set `axes`), one column per DA definition) from
137: `utils.pair_agreement`; [`da_per_benchmark.py`](da_per_benchmark.py)
138: melts it into a long (language, benchmark, comparison) table and the wide
139: `_size` / `_ckpt` pivots, and rewrites the deck's appendix slides for the
140: canonical pool. rq03 joins the SNR variants onto this table; rq05 asks the
141: complementary question — which proxy *size* ranks an intervention like the
142: reference, with languages as the population.
143: 
144: With few families at a size (18 A/B variants at 1.7B, 24 with every scheme;
145: a task in a language only the L50 mixture trains rests on three or four),
146: DA is quantised to 1/#pairs: read the
147: family-level averages, and `n` alongside every value
148: (`da_all_n_pairs_per_task_both_axes.csv`).

150: ## Departure from upstream: tie handling in `decision_acc_fast`
151: 
152: Upstream's kernel (`snr/metrics.py`, kept verbatim as
153: `decision_acc_fast_upstream`) compares `a > b` on both vectors, so a pair the
154: proxy ties but the target decides counts as an agreement or a disagreement
155: depending on which model is listed first — the value depends on the listing
156: order (alphabetical by family here), not on the benchmark. Since 2026-09-16
157: `decision_acc_fast` compares the sign of the difference on both sides
158: instead: tied in both agrees, tied in one and decided in the other is a miss,
159: and the result is order-invariant. Without ties the two are identical.
160: 
161: Measured on the `predictivity` pool at the final checkpoints (2026-09-16):
162: 3,301 of 119,456 family pairs are exact ties (2.76 %); 4.05 % of benchmark
163: pairs, where accuracy over a fixed item count ties easily, and none of the
164: per-language BPB or loss pairs; 1,225 of 2,845 (task, size) cells contain at
165: least one tie. The tables committed on 2026-09-16 were produced with the
166: upstream kernel and are regenerated by `run_all_predictivity.sh`.

168: ## Why the pooled panel keeps every scheme
169: 
170: The `all pairs` panel of `by_L.py` and the black `all pairs` line of
171: `scale_convergence.py` are one population: **every pair of design variants at the
172: grid seed (1904), every data scheme** — A, B, AT3, ZH and ES — cross-L and
173: cross-architecture included. Until 2026-09-22 both held the scheme to A and B, the
174: `predictivity` headline pool's filter, and that was wrong here for two reasons.
175: 
176: **It made the pooled panel a different population from the panels beside it.** The
177: per-L panels have always pooled the schemes (`L_POOL = predictivity_all` at the grid
178: seed), so L50's cell was comparing A against AT3 while the first panel, drawn on the
179: same axes and read as its pooled version, excluded exactly that comparison. The same
180: held between `scale_convergence`'s black line and its coloured ones.
181: 
182: **The reason for the A/B filter does not transfer.** The headline pool excludes AT3,
183: ZH and ES because they widen the *dispersion* an SNR signal is measured over without
184: widening the decision the pool exists to measure. Decision accuracy is a rank
185: agreement over a pair set: a temperature change or a swapped second language adds
186: decisions to that set, it does not inflate a spread. A practitioner choosing T = 1
187: over T = 3 is making a design decision exactly as much as one choosing deep over
188: shallow.
189: 
190: What it changes today: **123 of the 276 pairs at the reference** (24 families
191: against the 18 of schemes A and B): the four AT3 families (L15, L30 and L50 deep,
192: L50 shallow) and the two L2 second-language families (ZH, ES), all at 1.7B in the
193: 2026-09-23 report, against the A/B families and each other. Replicate seeds are
194: unaffected either way: they exist only
195: below the reference, so no pair containing one is ever comparable.
196: 
197: The consequence is not cosmetic. On the 2026-09-22 tables, pooled over the gated
198: benchmark tasks, the plain scale-convergence line across 175M → 1B went from
199: **0.590, 0.599, 0.600, 0.598** under the A/B filter to **0.586, 0.613, 0.623,
200: 0.629** with every scheme — a slope of **+0.010** against **+0.057** per decade of
201: non-embedding parameters; on the `above_66_both` population +0.100 against +0.180.
202: The A/B filter was suppressing measured convergence. On the 2026-09-23 tables
203: (the `rf_` twins at 175M and 350M, ZH and ES at 1.7B) the every-scheme line reads
204: **0.531, 0.536, 0.544, 0.557** (`scale_convergence_da_size_multi_axes.csv`, +0.034 per decade) and
205: 0.637 → 0.772 on `above_66_both` (+0.172); the A/B counterfactual has not been
206: re-measured on them.
207: 
208: The every-scheme numbers are `scale_convergence_da_size[_above_66_both]_multi_axes.csv` as it stands;
209: the A/B numbers are a counterfactual, since no A/B-only table is kept. Reproduce it
210: by restricting `pairs_by_group`'s OVERALL branch to
211: `{ra["scheme"], rb["scheme"]} <= {"A", "B"}` and rerunning — do not hand-carry these
212: numbers, which is how three of them were wrong in the first draft of this section.

214: ## What counts as one design axis
215: 
216: `scale_convergence.py --by transformation` draws one line per axis a pair differs
217: on, and drops any pair that moves two at once — such a pair is a decision about
218: neither. That only works if the axes are actually independent, and the `scheme`
219: token is not: it encodes **three** design choices at once. It is unpacked through
220: `DATA_SCHEMES` (`sets`, `temp`), the registry that defines the grid, so the axes
221: are `L`, `arch`, `list`, `T`, `lang2`, `en` (the English corpus of the L = 1 DCLMP/FWEB cells), `seed`:
222: 
223: | scheme | `list` | `T` | `lang2` | reading |
224: |---|---|---|---|---|
225: | A | A | 1 | ru | resource-ranked list, English + Russian at L = 2 |
226: | AT3 | A | 3 | ru | A's list sampled at T = 3 |
227: | B | B | 1 | ru | diversity-first list |
228: | BT3 | B | 3 | ru | B's list at T = 3 (registered 2026-09-21; no BT3 cell in the 2026-09-23 report) |
229: | ZH | A | 1 | zh | A's list with Chinese in the second slot |
230: | ES | A | 1 | es | A's list with Spanish in the second slot |
231: 
232: Two consequences, both of which were wrong before 2026-09-22:
233: 
234: **ZH and ES are not list designs.** At L = 2 every setting is "English + one other
235: language"; scheme A's second language is simply Russian. So A, ZH and ES share a
236: list and differ on `lang2` alone, and A's L2 cell is the `ru` level of the
237: second-language axis rather than a scheme of its own. They used to be three
238: separate one-pair groups; they are now one 3-pair axis, and all three reach the
239: reference in the 2026-09-23 report (ZH and ES at 1.7B) — exactly `MIN_PAIRS`.
240: `scale_convergence_da_size_transformation_multi_axes.csv` still draws no line for it: the axis is
241: 0.1 % of the pooled decisions (`share_lang2`).
242: 
243: **B vs AT3 moves two axes, not one.** Under the old `scheme` key it differed on a
244: single key and was dropped only because no label existed for the level pair — the
245: right outcome for the wrong reason, and the same accident hid ZH vs ES. Every axis
246: now has a label and nothing is dropped for want of one; a pair is dropped when, and
247: only when, it moves more than one axis.
248: 
249: At the reference the mono-axis pairs are `language count` 39, `depth` 10,
250: `language list` 6, `temperature` 4 (the L15, L30 and L50 AT3 cells against their
251: scheme-A twins; the line became drawable once the L15 and L30 AT3 cells reached
252: 1.7B) and `second language` 3; the other 214 of the 276 pairs move two or more
253: axes. When **BT3** lands, `B vs BT3` joins the temperature axis and `AT3 vs BT3`
254: the language-list axis with no code change.
