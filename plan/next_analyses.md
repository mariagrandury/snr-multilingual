# Next analyses — what the older experiments, the public models, FineTasks, the per-item logs and language transfer can still add (2026-09-23)

Written after the rq00–rq02 write-up (`src/signal-and-noise/analysis/rq02_decision_accuracy/pretraining/predictivity/README.md`) and a review of rq03–rq06, rq08 and the two older data sources. The paper's promise is to help a model developer decide **which benchmarks, in which languages, at which proxy size, read at which point of training** support a pretraining decision. Everything below is ranked by how directly it serves that promise against what it costs. Three items were implemented today (marked **done**); the rest are proposals with the data, the statistic and the pitfalls written down so the next session can start from the code.

## 0. The big picture, in five sentences

The above-random gate is the largest effect in the study and it is, for most gated tasks, a size/recipe floor rather than a benchmark floor: 25 of the 35 old-list tasks the ladder never reads are read by a public model of ≤ 1.7B (**done**, §2). The reformulated cloze twins move the same families across the gate at every ladder size, and the in-flight `rf_` evals are the experiment that decides how much of the four-option MCQ world comes back. Once a task is readable, ranking design variants from a smaller model is close to a coin flip on the full population (0.53 → 0.56), and DA-ckpt is mostly within-run persistence (real pairs 0.83 at 90 % of a 175M run, seed null 0.81); the honest developer advice is therefore about *which aggregate to read* (a panel macro beats English alone, **done**, §6) and *which item subsets to keep* (§5), not about which single benchmark is "reliable". FineTasks' selection criteria, computed on our ladder, pick tasks whose DA-size is 0.52–0.54 against 0.45–0.47 for the rest, so a criterion that never sees a larger model buys about a third of the gap between chance and the reference (**done**, §4). The older 36-model sweep and the public models are useful as *replications and controls* of these findings, never as rows in the same table (§1, §2).

## 1. The old custom grid (36-model sweep, pre-July)

**What it is.** `apertus-{175M,350M,600M,1B}-fwEdu{30,60,90}-fw2{..}-seed{28,1797,1904}`: 4 sizes × 3 English shares × 3 seeds, 103 B tokens at every size, 86 parent tasks, 10–16 checkpoints per run (6 common to all 36), `acc` only, no BPB, mixed backends (113 of 468 cells on `megatron_lm`, all seed 1904; the rest vLLM), reference 1B. Parquet `multilingual-snr/multilingual-snr-eval-results` (now cached under `src/signal-and-noise/data/multilingual_snr/`, gitignored); checkpoints on the HF org `snr-models`. Committed outputs on the `seeds_*` / `custom_swissai_hf` pools predate rule 15 and pair seed replicates as design variants.

**Why it cannot be pooled with the ladder.** Harness at GitHub HEAD per job (unrecoverable), backend split, `acc` on lettered originals against `acc_norm` on cloze twins, no BPB, 103 B tokens flat against D = 100·N, a different checkpoint grid, reference 1B against 1.7B. The root `CLAUDE.md` now says so.

**What it can still add (ranked).**

1. **A1 — the DA-ckpt seed null at every cell of a 3-seed grid.** The ladder's claim "DA-ckpt ≈ seed null" rests on 11 replicated cells and has no 350M or 1.7B null. The sweep has three seeds at all 12 (size, mix) cells: 9 null pairs per size at four sizes, DA on a 1/9 lattice. Blockers, all verified: `compute_da.py` calls `design_axes` unconditionally and it raises on the parquet frame (no `L`/`arch`/`scheme`; map `mix_en` → an axis, `seed` → seed); `snr.target_size` = 1.7B leaves the DA-size columns empty (accept DA-ckpt only); the `seeds_*` pools carry `include_external: true` since 56c806d and return 18 families up to 70B (filter to `apertus-*`). Restrict null pairs to vLLM-scored cells so a (1904, 28) pair is not also a backend pair. Half a day of code, minutes of compute. Frame it as a replication appendix.
2. **A2 — a 3-seed test-retest ceiling for DA-size per task**, same plumbing; 3 design pairs per size, so a pooled-over-tasks ceiling only.
3. **A3 — English-share decisions against language-count decisions**: the committed rq04 rankings already show the SNR-family transfer (ρ 0.83 under DA-ckpt, −0.71 under DA-size); one appendix paragraph, no computation.
4. **A4 — re-evaluate the 36 checkpoints on the pinned harness with the twins and BPB**: removes every incompatibility; ~468 eval jobs; only under reviewer pressure and with per-launch approval.

**B3 — the reference models as a ranking oracle for the ladder's benchmarks** (exploratory): do the public models of 1–3B order the *benchmarks* by difficulty the way the ladder's 1.7B does (Spearman of per-task mean score across the two tiers)? ## 4. FineTasks

**What they do.** 49 single-seed 1.5B recipes (30 B tokens, ~1 B-token checkpoints), 185 candidate tasks in 9 languages → 96 selected by four criteria: monotonicity (mean over runs of Spearman ρ(step, score) ≥ 0.5), "low noise" (mean final score over runs / mean over steps of the score's std *across* runs > 20), non-random (best run − baseline > 3 × final cross-run std) and ordering consistency (Kendall τ between consecutive steps after 15 B tokens). Their "noise" is what the SNR framework calls *signal*. They select on the cloze (CF) formulation and evaluate the leaderboard on MCF; they choose the scoring rule per task (acc / char-norm / token-norm / PMI).

**Overlap.** 45 of their 96 tasks have a benchmark+language counterpart in our registry; every letter-format pick (all MMLU and Belebele variants) is at chance on our ladder in its original form and passes only as its `rf_` twin; their NLU picks (hellaswag, xstorycloze, xnli, xcopa) are exactly our gate survivors. Table: `analysis/rq09_benchmark_design/finetasks_overlap.csv`.

**Their criteria on our ladder (done).** `analysis/rq09_benchmark_design/finetasks_criteria.py` computes the four statistics per (task, size) on the 18 design variants along the ten tenths and judges them by DA-size against 1.7B, which they never see. Findings (tables of the 06:16 snapshot): the composite passes 13 % of tasks at 175M and 38 % at 1B (our gate: 31 % → 49 %); their SNR > 20 passes about 60 % of tasks at every size and does not discriminate, monotonicity is the size-sensitive criterion; passers have DA-size 0.52–0.54 at every proxy against 0.45–0.47 for failers; each statistic correlates with DA-size at Spearman 0.10–0.39 across tasks, the same range as rq03's 22 SNR definitions (best `dispersion_shifted` 0.28–0.38; the figure's panel e). Of their picks present here, 49 % of the originals pass their own criteria on our ladder against 100 % of the cloze twins. Reading for the paper: a selection rule computed on one size without a reference recovers about a third of the chance-to-reference gap; the format decision (cloze) matters more than any of the four thresholds.

**Follow-ups.** (a) Their per-task statistics are downloadable (`analysis/data/nanotron_tasks/<lang>/*_stats.csv` in the Space): correlate their ρ/τ/SNR with ours on the 45 shared tasks, not only the verdicts. (b) The scoring-rule axis: the harness logs carry `acc` and `acc_norm` for every cloze task, so a re-melt of `eval_logs/` gives the length-normalisation axis at no eval cost; PMI needs a second pass. (c) Their category-balanced per-language aggregate with baseline rescaling is a better per-language figure than ours. (d) What ours adds and the paper should say: DA against a larger reference, the seed null that their consecutive-step τ lacks, gate survivorship across five sizes, 22 SNR definitions ranked against DA, and paired reformulation twins on identical models.

## 5. rq08 — per-item selection on the ladder

**What exists.** Subtask-level selection with a selection null (ladder, no holdout: median gain +0.28 per benchmark, 33/75 cells beat the null's p95). The per-item "Option D" family reads `samples_*.jsonl` and ran on the 36-sweep only: subset beats full in 100 % of cells by construction, median gain +0.64, but cross-size Jaccard 0.03 → "item ranking is mostly noise". Its loader keys checkpoints without the seed (two of three seeds overwrote each other), points at an eval root that no longer exists, and only matches `apertus-*` names.

**The data is there, for the ladder.** iopsstor `msnr/`: 4,356 checkpoint dirs of 156 cells, ≥ 2,439 with samples, ≥ 4.1 M samples files, ≈ 10.4 TB; every 1.7B final has them; capstor mirrors 2,988 dirs. Records carry `doc_id`, `acc`, `acc_norm` and the per-choice log-likelihoods, so a continuous per-item score (gold-choice LL, top-two margin) exists without re-evaluation. The 36-sweep samples are gone from both filesystems.

**Plan (ranked).** P1, a per-item store (a Slurm job, 1–2 h, needs approval): walk the eval dirs, keep the shared-grid checkpoints and `auto` trained tasks, extract `(model, step, task, doc_id, acc, acc_norm, ll_gold, margin)` by regex on the record head, parquet partitioned by family, ≈ 0.6 G rows / 2–4 GB, resumable. P2, port Option D on the rule-4 noise window with the selection null. P3, the protocol that makes it publishable: select items on half of the seed-1904 families at proxy sizes, score DA of the subset mean on the other half against 1.7B (mono-axis pairs), swap; seed holdout on `predictivity_seeds_train/test` for SNR; cross-size transfer with 27 families instead of 3 mixes. P4, greedy and IRT only where D transfers. Findings to aim for: the fraction of "dead" items (identical outcome for every proxy model) per benchmark/language/size; the smallest item fraction keeping ≥ 95 % of the full-set DA on held-out families; whether informative items transfer across sizes; `rf_` vs original at item level (doc_id-aligned); whether continuous LL scores beat binary accuracy for the same subset.

## 6. rq06 — language transfer, new angles

**Why the cross-task maps are empty.** `cross_task.py` asks for DA ≥ 0.75 sustained over sizes on a population whose own-task DA is 0.55–0.61, then needs more than half the pairs to reach it: 2.6 % of off-diagonal cells reach a level, 0 of 1,124 by language. The DA matrices that would show a graded result (`size_da`, `ckpt_da`) are computed and never written.

**The minimal language panel (done).** `analysis/rq06_language_transfer/language_panel.py`: per benchmark family, the 1.7B MACRO ranking over the L8 panel languages readable at 1.7B, recovered by a proxy that reads one language, English, or its own macro. Pooled over 12 benchmarks at 1B: English alone 0.59, the other single languages 0.54–0.67 (de 0.67, ru 0.65, zh 0.64, it 0.64, es 0.62, fr 0.61, ja 0.54), the panel macro 0.66; per benchmark the macro reaches 0.93 (hellaswag), 0.89 (LAMBADA, INCLUDE-rfgm), 0.81 (Global-MMLU-rf) at 1B where English reads 0.87, 0.78, —, 0.69. English is the worst single language on the pooled panel at 600M and 1B. Reading: an English-only proxy misreads the multilingual decision; the panel's errors are partly independent, so the macro is worth its cost. Follow-ups: greedy k-language panels (which two or three languages recover the macro), the same against the macro over ALL trained languages per L (requires per-L pairs, rule 2), and the seed null as the floor.

**Other angles, ranked.** (B) BPB in a language as the proxy for the same language's benchmark decisions: write `cross_task.py`'s `size_da` matrix to CSV, slice `bpb_<lang>` columns against the language's gated benchmark rows, mean DA per language and size (a sanity check gave Spearman 0.41 between `bpb_<lang>` DA-size and the language's mean benchmark DA-size at 1B, −0.07 at 175M). ~40 lines. (C) tier → tier transfer: the ranking on high-resource benchmarks at the proxy against the 1.7B ranking on low-resource ones, a 4×4 tier matrix pooled with `jackknife_ratio`; thin at L50 (6 pairs). ~80 lines. (E) fix rq06's α pool: it pools the exponent over all 99 other languages (mostly never-trained ones) and the "transfer beats own fit" result for trained languages is a curvature artefact of the 4-rung own fit; pool over trained languages, add a curved own fit, and state the 2–7 % BPB error in units of the design effect (0.6 %), which makes clear the transfer predicts a level, not a decision. Also drop the stale decision-transfer figures (`transfer_da_lines.csv` is empty; `transfer_da_by_L.*` from a removed population). (D) benchmark-side zero-shot transfer to untrained languages: only the deep-A cells carry untrained evals, so it is a language-count decision on 15 pairs; a limitation paragraph unless the shallow/B cells get the 105-language eval.

## 7. Review findings in rq03–rq05 worth fixing before the paper

- rq04 ranks the 22 SNR variants against the mean of DA-size and DA-ckpt; the rankings agree (ρ 0.84–0.91) but the argmax is not identifiable (`aad` 0.205 vs `quartile_deviation` 0.206), and the SNR–DA-ckpt correlation is largest at the late fractions where DA-ckpt is persistence (seed null 0.77 vs real 0.81 at f90). Recommend the family, add the seed-null line to the r-by-fraction figure, and quote the pooled n≈150–190 correlations (ρ 0.02–0.24) rather than per-language means.
- rq03: every SNR variant uses checkpoint noise, which is 1.8× smaller than seed noise (median ratio over 794 cells); the seed holdout's per-language metrics rest on one language.
- rq05: the benchmark population is not gated (`_population()` returns every benchmark row) while `transformations.py` gates; `n_decided` = 0 for depth on BPB in every cell, yet the highlight says 175M reaches DA ≥ 0.75; `early_decision.py` reports the first fraction reaching 0.75 rather than a sustained one; `seed_sd` from 3–4 cells is applied to the 1.7B reference.
- rq02 `cross_task.py`: write the DA matrices; replace the sustained-0.75 level maps with graded mean-DA maps.
